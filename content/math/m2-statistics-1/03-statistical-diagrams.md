## Lesson

A good diagram shows the pattern in data at a glance. Choose the diagram to
suit the data.

### Bar charts

A **bar chart** compares the frequencies of categories or discrete values.

- One axis lists the categories; the other shows frequency, starting at **0**.
- Bars are of **equal width**, with **equal gaps** between them.
- Label both axes and give the chart a title.

Bar charts suit qualitative or discrete data: favourite sports, number of
siblings.

A **pictograph** does the same job with symbols, where each symbol stands for a
stated number of items (a key is essential). Half a symbol stands for half
that number.

### Pie charts

A **pie chart** shows how a whole is **shared** between categories. The whole
circle, 360°, represents the total.

$$\text{angle for a category} = \frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}} \times 360°$$

> 60 students chose their favourite fruit: mango 25, banana 15, apple 12,
> orange 8.

| Fruit | Frequency | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Mango | 25 | $\frac{25}{60} \times 360° = 150°$ |
| Banana | 15 | $\frac{15}{60} \times 360° = 90°$ |
| Apple | 12 | $\frac{12}{60} \times 360° = 72°$ |
| Orange | 8 | $\frac{8}{60} \times 360° = 48°$ |
| **Total** | **60** | **360°** |

**Check:** the angles must add to 360°.

A shortcut: $360 \div 60 = 6°$ per student, then multiply each frequency by 6.

**Reading a pie chart backwards:** a sector of 72° out of a total of 60 people
represents $\frac{72}{360} \times 60 = 12$ people.

### Line graphs

A **line graph** shows how something **changes over time** — rainfall each
month, a patient's temperature each hour. Plot points and join them with
straight lines. Time goes on the horizontal axis.

### Histograms

A **histogram** looks like a bar chart but is for **continuous grouped** data.
There are **no gaps** between the bars, because the classes run on into each
other: a class of $2 \leq m < 4$ ends exactly where $4 \leq m < 6$ begins.
With equal class widths, bar height shows frequency.

### Analysing diagrams

When a question asks you to interpret a diagram:

- Read the **scale** carefully — it may go up in 2s, 5s or 10s.
- Look for the **largest** and **smallest** categories, and any trend.
- Compare using **numbers** from the diagram, not vague words.
- Watch for **misleading** diagrams: an axis that doesn't start at 0 makes small
  differences look huge.

## Flashcards

### 1.3 | How do you find the angle for a category in a pie chart?
$$\frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}} \times 360°$$

### 1.3 | What must the angles in a pie chart add up to?
360°.

### 1.3 | What is the difference between a bar chart and a histogram?
A histogram is for **continuous** grouped data and has **no gaps** between the
bars.

### 1.3 | Which diagram best shows a change over time?
A **line graph**.

### 1.7 | A 90° sector in a pie chart of 200 people represents how many?
50. $\frac{90}{360} = \frac{1}{4}$, and a quarter of 200 is 50.

### 1.7 | Why can a bar chart with an axis starting at 50 be misleading?
It exaggerates small differences between the bars.

## Questions

### 1.3 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
In a survey of 90 people, 30 chose red. What angle represents red on a pie
chart, in degrees?

**Answer:** 120

**Working:** $\frac{30}{90} \times 360° = \frac{1}{3} \times 360° = 120°$.

### 1.7 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A pie chart represents 240 students. The sector for "walks to school" has an
angle of 75°. How many students walk?

**Answer:** 50

**Working:** $\frac{75}{360} \times 240 = 50$.

### 1.3 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which diagram is **most** suitable for showing the heights of 50 students,
grouped into classes such as $150 \leq h < 155$?

- A. Pie chart
- B. Pictograph
- C. Histogram
- D. Line graph

**Answer:** C

**Working:** Height is continuous and the data is grouped, so a histogram (no
gaps between bars) is the right choice.

### 1.7 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A pie chart shows how Ria spends her \$1 800 monthly income. The sector for
rent is 144°. The sector for food is half the size of the rent sector. How much
does she spend on food?

- A. \$360
- B. \$400
- C. \$720
- D. \$900

**Answer:** A

**Working:** Food: $144° \div 2 = 72°$.
$\frac{72}{360} \times 1800 = 360$.

C is the rent.

### 1.3 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 3
The table shows how 120 students travel to school.

| Method | Walk | Bus | Car | Bicycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Students | 42 | 36 | 30 | 12 |

(a) Calculate the angle for each sector of a pie chart. **(3 marks)**

(b) What percentage of students travel by car? **(1 mark)**

(c) Give one reason why a pie chart suits this data. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) Walk 126°, Bus 108°, Car 90°, Bicycle 36° (b) 25%
(c) It shows how the whole group is shared between the four methods.

**Working:** (a) $360 \div 120 = 3°$ per student. $42 \times 3 = 126$,
$36 \times 3 = 108$, $30 \times 3 = 90$, $12 \times 3 = 36$. Check:
$126 + 108 + 90 + 36 = 360$. ✓ (3 marks for all four, 2 for three.)

(b) $\frac{30}{120} \times 100\% = 25\%$.
