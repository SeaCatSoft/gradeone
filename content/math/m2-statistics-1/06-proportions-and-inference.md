## Lesson

### Proportion above or below a value

Count how many values meet the condition, then write that as a fraction or
percentage of the total.

$$\text{percentage} = \frac{\text{number meeting the condition}}{\text{total}} \times 100\%$$

> The marks of 20 students: 45, 52, 38, 71, 66, 59, 80, 49, 55, 62, 74, 41,
> 58, 67, 50, 35, 69, 77, 53, 60. What percentage scored **50 or more**?

Values below 50: 45, 38, 49, 41, 35 — five of them. So $20 - 5 = 15$ scored 50
or more:

$$\frac{15}{20} \times 100\% = 75\%$$

Counting the **smaller** group and subtracting is often quicker and less
error-prone.

Read the wording precisely: "more than 50" excludes 50; "at least 50" and "50 or
more" include it.

### From a frequency table

Add the frequencies of the values that meet the condition.

| Hours of sleep | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Students | 3 | 8 | 12 | 10 | 7 |

Total: 40. Fewer than 7 hours: $3 + 8 = 11$ students, which is
$\frac{11}{40} \times 100\% = 27.5\%$.

A cumulative frequency column makes "fewer than" questions quick to read.

### Making inferences

An **inference** is a conclusion about a population, drawn from a sample. A good
inference:

- is **based on the numbers** — quote them
- stays **within what the data shows**
- remembers it comes from a **sample**, so it's an estimate, not a certainty

> In a random sample of 50 students, 32 said they use a phone for homework.

A reasonable inference: "About 64% of students in the school use a phone for
homework." For a school of 800, that suggests about $0.64 \times 800 = 512$
students.

An **unjustified** claim: "Using a phone improves homework marks." The survey
didn't measure marks at all.

Be cautious when the sample is **small** or **biased**. A survey of 5 people, or
only of people at a phone shop, doesn't support conclusions about everyone.

## Flashcards

### 1.9 | How do you find the percentage of values above a given value?
Count the values above it, divide by the total, and multiply by 100%.

### 1.9 | Does "at least 60" include 60?
Yes. "At least 60" means 60 or more.

### 1.11 | What is an inference?
A conclusion about a population, drawn from sample data.

### 1.11 | 30% of a random sample of 40 students walk to school. Estimate how many walk, out of 900.
About 270. $0.30 \times 900 = 270$.

### 1.11 | Name two reasons an inference might be unreliable.
The sample is too **small**, or it is **biased**.

## Questions

### 1.9 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
The heights, in cm, of 10 plants are 12, 18, 9, 25, 14, 20, 7, 16, 22, 11.
How many plants are taller than 15 cm?

**Answer:** 5

**Working:** 18, 25, 20, 16 and 22: five plants.

### 1.9 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
The table shows the scores of 50 players.

| Score | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 14 | 15 | 10 | 5 |

What percentage of players scored **more than** 3?

**Answer:** 30

**Working:** More than 3 means 4 or 5: $10 + 5 = 15$ players.
$\frac{15}{50} \times 100\% = 30\%$.

### 1.11 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
In a random sample of 200 voters, 88 support Party A. There are 15 000 voters
in the town. Which is the best estimate of the number of supporters of Party A
in the town?

- A. 88
- B. 1 320
- C. 6 600
- D. 8 400

**Answer:** C

**Working:** Sample proportion: $\frac{88}{200} = 0.44$. Estimate:
$0.44 \times 15\,000 = 6600$.

### 1.11 | structured | 4 marks | difficulty 3
A reporter stands outside a gym and asks 25 people whether they exercise at
least three times a week. 21 say yes. The reporter writes: "84% of people in
this country exercise at least three times a week."

(a) Show how the reporter got 84%. **(1 mark)**

(b) Give **two** reasons why the claim is unreliable. **(2 marks)**

(c) Suggest how the survey could be improved. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) $\frac{21}{25} \times 100\% = 84\%$ (b) The sample is biased —
people outside a gym are more likely to exercise; and 25 is a very small sample
for a whole country. (c) Survey a much larger random sample drawn from the
whole population.

**Working:** (b) 1 mark for each valid reason.
