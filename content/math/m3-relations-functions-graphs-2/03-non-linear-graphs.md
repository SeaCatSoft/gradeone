## Lesson

Not every graph is a straight line. Each family of functions has its own
recognisable shape.

### Quadratic: $y = ax^2 + bx + c$

A **parabola** — U-shaped if $a > 0$, ∩-shaped if $a < 0$. Covered in Module 2.

The graph is a **hyperbola**: two separate curves in opposite quarters of the
plane. Key features:

- There is **no value** at $x = 0$ — you can't divide by zero.
- As $x$ gets large, $y$ gets close to 0 but never reaches it. The axes are
  **asymptotes**: lines the curve approaches but never touches.
- With $k > 0$ the branches are in the top-right and bottom-left; with $k < 0$
  they're in the other two corners.

### Drawing non-linear graphs

1. Make a table of values, including negatives.
2. Plot carefully; the shape depends on getting the signs right.
3. Join with a **smooth curve**.
4. For a reciprocal, do **not** join the two branches across the $y$-axis — and
   use extra values near zero (like $x = 0.5$), where the curve changes fastest.

### Interpreting a graph

From a drawn graph you can read:

- **Roots** — where it crosses the $x$-axis, the solutions of $f(x) = 0$.
- **Solutions of $f(x) = k$** — where the curve meets the horizontal line
  $y = k$.
- **Maximum and minimum points** — the peaks and troughs.
- **Where the function is increasing or decreasing** — where the curve rises or
  falls as you move right.
- **Intersections with another graph** — the solutions of the two equations
  together.

## Flashcards

### 2.4 | What shape is the graph of a cubic function?
An **S-shape**. It can cross the $x$-axis up to three times.

### 2.4 | What shape is the graph of $y = \frac{k}{x}$?
A **hyperbola**: two separate branches in opposite quarters.

### 2.4 | Why is there a gap in the graph of $y = \frac{6}{x}$ at $x = 0$?
Division by zero is undefined, so the function has no value there.

### 2.4 | What is an asymptote?
A line that a curve approaches but never touches, such as the axes for
$y = \frac{k}{x}$.

### 2.5 | How do you solve $f(x) = 3$ from a graph of $y = f(x)$?
Draw the line $y = 3$ and read the $x$-coordinates where it meets the curve.

## Questions

### 2.4 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which equation gives an S-shaped curve?

- A. $y = 2x + 1$
- B. $y = x^2 - 4$
- C. $y = x^3 - 2x$
- D. $y = \frac{4}{x}$

**Answer:** C

**Working:** The highest power is 3, so it's a cubic, whose graph is S-shaped.

### 2.4 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
For $y = x^3 - 4x$, find the value of $y$ when $x = -2$.

**Answer:** 0

**Working:** $(-2)^3 - 4(-2) = -8 + 8 = 0$. So $x = -2$ is a root.

### 2.4 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which statement about the graph of $y = \frac{12}{x}$ is true?

- A. It crosses the $x$-axis at 12.
- B. It passes through the origin.
- C. It has two branches and never meets the axes.
- D. It is a straight line.

**Answer:** C

**Working:** The value of $\frac{12}{x}$ is never 0 and is undefined at $x = 0$,
so the curve never touches either axis.

### 2.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
The graph of $y = f(x)$ crosses the $x$-axis at $-1$, 2 and 4, and has a maximum
at $(0.5, 6)$. How many solutions does $f(x) = 6$ have?

- A. 0
- B. 1
- C. 2
- D. 3

**Answer:** C

**Working:** The line $y = 6$ touches the maximum at $(0.5, 6)$, and the curve
rises back to 6 again beyond $x = 4$ (it is a cubic with three roots, so it
rises after the last one). That gives two intersections.

### 2.4 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 3
(a) Complete the table for $y = \dfrac{8}{x}$. **(2 marks)**

| $x$ | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | | | | |

(b) Draw the graph for $1 \leq x \leq 8$. **(2 marks)**

(c) Use your graph to estimate the value of $x$ for which $y = 3$. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) 8, 4, 2, 1 (b) A smooth curve falling steeply then flattening
(c) About 2.7

**Working:** (c) Read across from $y = 3$ to the curve, then down. Exactly:
$x = \frac{8}{3} = 2.67$.
