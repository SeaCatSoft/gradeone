## Lesson

### Angles of elevation and depression

- The **angle of elevation** is measured **up** from the horizontal.
- The **angle of depression** is measured **down** from the horizontal.

Both are measured from the **horizontal**, never from the vertical.

Because the horizontal lines at the top and bottom are parallel, the angle of
**depression** from the top of a tower to a boat equals the angle of
**elevation** from the boat to the top (alternate angles).

> From a point 50 m from the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top
> is 38°. Find the height of the tower.

The 50 m is **adjacent** to the angle; the height is **opposite**. So use
tangent:

$$\tan 38° = \frac{h}{50} \quad\Rightarrow\quad h = 50 \tan 38° = 39.1 \text{ m (1 d.p.)}$$

### Drawing the diagram

If a question has no diagram, **draw one**:

1. Sketch the situation with a right angle marked.
2. Put the given lengths and angles on it.
3. Label the side you want with a letter.
4. Decide on the ratio, using SOH CAH TOA.

Most lost marks in this topic come from a missing or mislabelled diagram —
especially putting the angle of depression inside the triangle at the wrong
vertex.

### Bearings

A **bearing** gives a direction as an angle:

- measured from **north**
- **clockwise**
- always written with **three figures**: 060°, 135°, 315°

North is 000°, east 090°, south 180° and west 270°.

The **back bearing** (the return direction) is the bearing $+ 180°$, or
$- 180°$ if that would exceed 360°. If $B$ is on a bearing of 070° from $A$, then
$A$ is on a bearing of 250° from $B$.

> A ship sails 8 km east, then 6 km north. How far is it from its start, and on
> what bearing?

Distance: $\sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10$ km.

For the bearing, the angle east of north satisfies $\tan \theta = \frac{8}{6}$,
so $\theta = 53.1°$. The bearing is **053°**.

Take care over which side is opposite the angle you want: bearings are measured
from **north**, so the north–south leg is usually the **adjacent** side.

### Multi-step problems

Harder questions need two triangles, or Pythagoras and then trigonometry. Work
one triangle at a time, and carry unrounded values between steps — round only
at the end.

## Flashcards

### 4.10 | What is the angle of elevation measured from?
The **horizontal**, looking upwards.

### 4.10 | How is the angle of depression from a cliff to a boat related to the angle of elevation from the boat?
They are **equal** (alternate angles between parallel horizontals).

### 4.10 | How are bearings measured?
From **north**, **clockwise**, written with three figures.

### 4.10 | What is the bearing of due west?
270°.

### 4.10 | $B$ is on a bearing of 115° from $A$. What is the bearing of $A$ from $B$?
295°. Add 180°.

### 4.10 | Which ratio connects an angle of elevation with a horizontal distance and a height?
**Tangent**: the height is opposite and the horizontal distance is adjacent.

## Questions

### 4.10 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
From a point 30 m from the base of a building, the angle of elevation of the
top is 55°. Find the height of the building, in metres, to 1 decimal place.

**Answer:** 42.8

**Working:** $\tan 55° = \frac{h}{30}$, so $h = 30 \tan 55° = 42.84 \approx 42.8$ m.

### 4.10 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A kite is on a string 40 m long, at an angle of elevation of 62°. How high is
the kite above the ground, in metres, to 1 decimal place? (Ignore the height of
the person holding it.)

**Answer:** 35.3

**Working:** The string is the hypotenuse, so use sine:
$h = 40 \sin 62° = 35.32 \approx 35.3$ m.

### 4.10 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A town $Q$ is on a bearing of 040° from town $P$. What is the bearing of $P$
from $Q$?

- A. 040°
- B. 140°
- C. 220°
- D. 320°

**Answer:** C

**Working:** Back bearing: $40° + 180° = 220°$.

### 4.10 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
From the top of a cliff 80 m high, the angle of depression of a boat is 25°.
How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff, in metres, to the nearest metre?

**Answer:** 172

**Working:** The angle of elevation from the boat is also 25°. The height is
opposite and the distance adjacent, so $\tan 25° = \frac{80}{d}$ and
$d = \frac{80}{\tan 25°} = 171.6 \approx 172$ m.

### 4.10 | structured | 6 marks | difficulty 4
A ship leaves port $P$ and sails 15 km on a bearing of 090° to point $Q$. It
then sails 9 km due north to point $R$.

(a) Draw a sketch of the journey. **(1 mark)**

(b) Calculate the distance $PR$, to 1 decimal place. **(2 marks)**

(c) Calculate the bearing of $R$ from $P$, to the nearest degree. **(3 marks)**

**Answer:** (b) 17.5 km (c) 059°

**Working:** (a) A right angle at $Q$, with $PQ$ east and $QR$ north.

(b) $PR = \sqrt{15^2 + 9^2} = \sqrt{306} = 17.49 \approx 17.5$ km (2 marks).

(c) The angle at $P$ between north and $PR$: the east leg (15) is opposite and
the north leg (9) is adjacent, so $\tan \theta = \frac{15}{9}$ (1 mark),
giving $\theta = 59.04°$ (1 mark). The bearing is **059°** (1 mark).
