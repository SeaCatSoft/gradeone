## Lesson

A **construction** uses only a straight edge and a pair of compasses — no
protractor. **Leave all your construction arcs showing.** They are the evidence
that you constructed rather than measured, and they earn the marks.

### Perpendicular bisector of a line segment $AB$

This line cuts $AB$ in half at right angles.

1. Open the compasses to **more than half** of $AB$.
2. With the point on $A$, draw arcs above and below the line.
3. Without changing the radius, repeat from $B$, so the arcs cross twice.
4. Rule a line through the two crossing points.

Every point on this line is **equidistant** from $A$ and $B$.

### Bisecting an angle

1. With the point on the vertex, draw an arc crossing **both** arms.
2. From each of those two crossing points, draw arcs (same radius) that cross
   each other inside the angle.
3. Rule a line from the vertex through that crossing.

### Constructing 60°

1. Draw a line and mark the point $A$ on it.
2. With the point on $A$, draw a large arc crossing the line at $B$.
3. With the point on $B$ (same radius), draw an arc crossing the first arc at
   $C$.
4. Join $A$ to $C$. Angle $CAB = 60°$.

It works because $A$, $B$ and $C$ make an **equilateral triangle** (all sides
equal to the radius), and each of its angles is 60°.

### Other angles

- **30°:** construct 60°, then bisect it.
- **90°:** construct the perpendicular at a point — or bisect a straight angle
  (180°).
- **45°:** construct 90°, then bisect it.
- **120°:** construct 60° twice, side by side.

### Perpendicular from a point to a line

From a point $P$ not on the line: draw an arc centred on $P$ that crosses the
line twice, then construct the perpendicular bisector of those two crossing
points. It passes through $P$.

### Constructing a triangle from three sides

> Construct triangle $ABC$ with $AB = 7$ cm, $BC = 5$ cm, $AC = 6$ cm.

1. Rule $AB = 7$ cm.
2. Set the compasses to 6 cm; from $A$, draw an arc.
3. Set them to 5 cm; from $B$, draw an arc crossing the first. That is $C$.
4. Join $AC$ and $BC$.

## Flashcards

### 4.4 | What instruments are allowed in a construction?
A straight edge (ruler) and a pair of compasses — no protractor.

### 4.4 | Why must construction arcs be left on the page?
They show the method, and they earn the marks.

### 4.4 | How do you construct an angle of 30°?
Construct 60°, then bisect it.

### 4.4 | Why does the 60° construction work?
The three points form an equilateral triangle, whose angles are all 60°.

### 4.4 | What is special about points on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$?
They are the same distance from $A$ and from $B$.

## Questions

### 4.4 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which angle can be constructed by bisecting a 90° angle?

- A. 30°
- B. 45°
- C. 60°
- D. 120°

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Half of 90° is 45°.

### 4.4 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
When constructing the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, the compasses must be set
to

- A. exactly half of $AB$
- B. less than half of $AB$
- C. more than half of $AB$
- D. exactly $AB$

**Answer:** C

**Working:** With less than half, the arcs from $A$ and $B$ wouldn't meet. With
exactly half they would only touch at the midpoint.

### 4.4 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
An angle of 75° can be constructed by

- A. bisecting 150°
- B. bisecting 60°, then bisecting again
- C. constructing 60° and 30° side by side
- D. bisecting 120°

**Answer:** A

**Working:** 150° is 90° + 60° side by side, and half of 150° is 75°.

C gives 90°, D gives 60°, and B gives 15°.

### 4.4 | structured | 4 marks | difficulty 3
Describe how to construct triangle $PQR$ in which $PQ = 8$ cm, angle
$QPR = 60°$ and $PR = 5$ cm, using a ruler and compasses only. **(4 marks)**

**Answer:** Rule $PQ = 8$ cm (1 mark). At $P$, construct a 60° angle: arc
centred on $P$ crossing $PQ$, then an arc of the same radius centred where it
crosses $PQ$, and join $P$ to the intersection (2 marks). Mark $R$ on that line
5 cm from $P$ using the compasses, then join $QR$ (1 mark).
