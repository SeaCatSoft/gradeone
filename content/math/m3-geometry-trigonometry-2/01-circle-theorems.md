## Lesson

Circle theorems are a small set of facts about angles in circles. As with all
geometry at CSEC, **state the theorem you use** — the reason carries a mark.

### The theorems

**1. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference**, when
both stand on the same arc.

In the diagram, angle $AOB = 2 \times$ angle $APB$.

**2. The angle in a semicircle is 90°.** If $AB$ is a **diameter** and $P$ is on
the circle, angle $APB = 90°$. (It follows from theorem 1: the angle at the
centre is the straight angle 180°.)

**3. Angles in the same segment are equal.** Any two angles at the
circumference standing on the **same arc** are equal.

**4. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.** A cyclic
quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circle.

**5. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius** at the point of contact.

**6. Tangents from an external point are equal in length.** The two tangents
from a point outside the circle meet the circle at equal distances, making an
isosceles triangle.

**7. The alternate segment theorem.** The angle between a tangent and a chord
equals the angle in the **alternate** (opposite) segment.

### Using them

Most questions chain two or three theorems. A method that works:

1. Mark everything you know on the diagram.
2. Look for the **key features**: a diameter (think 90°), a tangent (think 90°
   with the radius, or the alternate segment), four points on the circle
   (cyclic quadrilateral).
3. Work out one angle at a time, **writing the reason** beside each.
4. Use ordinary angle facts too — angles in a triangle, isosceles triangles.

**Two radii always make an isosceles triangle.** That fact solves more circle
problems than any theorem, because the base angles are equal.

> $O$ is the centre, $A$ and $B$ are on the circle, and angle $OAB = 35°$. Find
> angle $AOB$.

$OA = OB$ (radii), so triangle $OAB$ is isosceles and angle $OBA = 35°$.
Angles in a triangle: $180° - 35° - 35° = 110°$.

## Flashcards

### 3.1 | What is the relationship between the angle at the centre and the angle at the circumference on the same arc?
The angle at the centre is **twice** the angle at the circumference.

### 3.1 | What is the angle in a semicircle?
90°.

### 3.1 | What do opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to?
180°.

### 3.1 | What angle does a tangent make with the radius at the point of contact?
90°.

### 3.1 | State the alternate segment theorem.
The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate
segment.

### 3.1 | Why is a triangle formed by two radii always isosceles?
All radii of a circle are equal, so two of its sides are equal.

## Questions

### 3.1 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
The angle at the centre of a circle standing on arc $AB$ is 130°. Find the
angle at the circumference standing on the same arc, in degrees.

**Answer:** 65

**Working:** The angle at the circumference is half the angle at the centre:
$130 \div 2 = 65$.

### 3.1 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
In a cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$, angle $A = 105°$. Find angle $C$, in degrees.

**Answer:** 75

**Working:** Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°:
$180 - 105 = 75$.

### 3.1 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
$PQ$ is a diameter of a circle and $R$ is another point on the circle. Angle
$RPQ = 32°$. What is angle $PQR$?

- A. 32°
- B. 58°
- C. 64°
- D. 90°

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Angle $PRQ = 90°$ (angle in a semicircle), so
$180° - 90° - 32° = 58°$.

### 3.1 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
$O$ is the centre of a circle, and $T$ is a point outside it. $TA$ is a tangent
touching the circle at $A$. If angle $ATO = 27°$, find angle $AOT$, in degrees.

**Answer:** 63

**Working:** Angle $OAT = 90°$ (tangent perpendicular to radius), so
$180° - 90° - 27° = 63°$.

### 3.1 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 3
$A$, $B$ and $C$ are points on a circle with centre $O$. Angle $BAC = 40°$ and
$OB = OC$.

(a) Find angle $BOC$, giving a reason. **(2 marks)**

(b) Find angle $OBC$, giving a reason. **(2 marks)**

(c) State the size of the **reflex** angle $BOC$. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) 80° (b) 50° (c) 280°

**Working:** (a) The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the
circumference: $2 \times 40° = 80°$.

(b) Triangle $OBC$ is isosceles (radii $OB = OC$), so its base angles are
equal: $\frac{180° - 80°}{2} = 50°$.

(c) Angles at a point add to 360°: $360° - 80° = 280°$.
