## Lesson

A **binary operation** takes **two** numbers and combines them into one. The
four you know — $+$, $-$, $\times$, $\div$ — are all binary operations. CSEC
questions invent new ones, using symbols like $*$, $\circ$, $\Delta$ or $\oplus$,
and define exactly what they do.

### Reading the definition

> $a * b = 3a - b$

This says: whatever number is on the **left** of $*$ takes the place of $a$,
and whatever is on the **right** takes the place of $b$. Then calculate.

$$4 * 5 = 3(4) - 5 = 12 - 5 = 7$$

$$5 * 4 = 3(5) - 4 = 15 - 4 = 11$$

So $4 * 5$ and $5 * 4$ give different answers. The order matters because the
definition treats $a$ and $b$ differently.

### Substituting carefully

Use brackets, especially with negative numbers:

> $p \circ q = p^2 + 2q$. Find $(-3) \circ 4$.

$$(-3)^2 + 2(4) = 9 + 8 = 17$$

### Two operations in one question

When the expression has brackets, work out the brackets first, just as with
ordinary arithmetic.

> $a * b = a + 2b$. Find $(1 * 2) * 3$.

- Inside the bracket: $1 * 2 = 1 + 2(2) = 5$
- Then: $5 * 3 = 5 + 2(3) = 11$

The result of the first step becomes the new $a$.

### Solving with a binary operation

Sometimes you know the answer and must find a missing number.

> $x * y = 2x + y$. Given $x * 3 = 15$, find $x$.

$$2x + 3 = 15 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2x = 12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 6$$

## Flashcards

### 5.5 | What is a binary operation?
A rule that combines **two** numbers to give one result.

### 5.5 | Given $a * b = a^2 - b$, find $3 * 4$.
$3^2 - 4 = 9 - 4 = 5$.

### 5.5 | With $a * b = 2a + b$, does $1 * 3$ equal $3 * 1$?
No. $1 * 3 = 2 + 3 = 5$, but $3 * 1 = 6 + 1 = 7$. The order matters.

### 5.5 | How do you evaluate $(2 * 3) * 4$?
Work out the bracket first. Its answer becomes the left-hand number for the
second step.

## Questions

### 5.5 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
The operation $*$ is defined by $a * b = 4a + b$. Evaluate $2 * 5$.

**Answer:** 13

**Working:** $4(2) + 5 = 8 + 5 = 13$.

### 5.5 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
The operation $\Delta$ is defined by $p \,\Delta\, q = pq - q^2$. Evaluate
$5 \,\Delta\, (-2)$.

**Answer:** -14

**Working:** $(5)(-2) - (-2)^2 = -10 - 4 = -14$.

### 5.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Given that $x \circ y = \dfrac{x + y}{x - y}$, find $6 \circ 2$.

- A. $\frac{1}{2}$
- B. $2$
- C. $4$
- D. $8$

**Answer:** B

**Working:** $\dfrac{6 + 2}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2$.

### 5.5 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
The operation $*$ is defined by $a * b = a + 3b$. Evaluate $(2 * 1) * 4$.

**Answer:** 17

**Working:** Bracket first: $2 * 1 = 2 + 3(1) = 5$.
Then $5 * 4 = 5 + 3(4) = 17$.

### 5.5 | structured | 4 marks | difficulty 3
The binary operation $\oplus$ is defined by $m \oplus n = m^2 - 3n$.

(a) Evaluate $4 \oplus 1$. **(1 mark)**

(b) Evaluate $1 \oplus 4$. **(1 mark)**

(c) Find the value of $k$ for which $k \oplus 2 = 10$, given that $k > 0$.
**(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) 13 (b) $-11$ (c) $k = 4$

**Working:** (a) $4^2 - 3(1) = 16 - 3 = 13$.

(b) $1^2 - 3(4) = 1 - 12 = -11$.

(c) $k^2 - 3(2) = 10$, so $k^2 = 16$ (1 mark). $k = \pm 4$, and since $k > 0$,
$k = 4$ (1 mark).
