## Lesson

**Expanding** removes brackets. **Factorising** puts them back. They are the
same fact read in opposite directions:

$$\underbrace{3(x + 4)}_{\text{factorised}} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \underbrace{3x + 12}_{\text{expanded}}$$

### Expanding a single bracket

Multiply **every** term inside by the term outside — the distributive law:

$$5(2x - 3) = 10x - 15 \qquad -2(a - 4) = -2a + 8 \qquad x(x + 7) = x^2 + 7x$$

Watch the sign when the outside term is negative: $-2 \times -4 = +8$.

Expand and simplify by collecting like terms afterwards:

$$3(x + 2) - 2(x - 5) = 3x + 6 - 2x + 10 = x + 16$$

### Expanding two brackets

Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second — four
products in all:

$$(x + 3)(x + 5) = x^2 + 5x + 3x + 15 = x^2 + 8x + 15$$

"FOIL" is one way to remember them: **F**irsts, **O**utsides, **I**nsides,
**L**asts. A grid works just as well:

| $\times$ | $x$ | $+5$ |
|---|---|---|
| $x$ | $x^2$ | $5x$ |
| $+3$ | $3x$ | $15$ |

Squaring a bracket means multiplying it by itself:

$$(x - 4)^2 = (x - 4)(x - 4) = x^2 - 8x + 16$$

**Not** $x^2 + 16$ or $x^2 - 16$. Both middle terms are easy to forget.

### Factorising: common factors

Find the **highest common factor** of all the terms — numbers and letters —
and take it outside:

$$6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3) \qquad 4a^2 - 10ab = 2a(2a - 5b)$$

Always check by expanding back. And take out the **highest** factor:
$4a^2 - 10ab = 2(2a^2 - 5ab)$ isn't fully factorised, because $a$ is still
common.

### Factorising by grouping

With four terms, pair them up and factorise each pair:

$$ax + ay + 3x + 3y = a(x + y) + 3(x + y) = (x + y)(a + 3)$$

The bracket $(x + y)$ is now a common factor itself.

### Difference of two squares

$$a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$$

Spot two perfect squares with a **minus** between them:

$$x^2 - 25 = (x + 5)(x - 5) \qquad 9y^2 - 4 = (3y + 2)(3y - 2)$$

A **sum** of two squares, like $x^2 + 25$, does not factorise this way.

### Trinomials $x^2 + bx + c$

Look for two numbers that **multiply** to $c$ and **add** to $b$.

$x^2 + 7x + 12$: which pair multiplies to 12 and adds to 7? $3$ and $4$.

$$x^2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4)$$

$x^2 - 2x - 15$: multiply to $-15$, add to $-2$. That's $-5$ and $+3$.

$$x^2 - 2x - 15 = (x - 5)(x + 3)$$

Check by expanding. The signs are where mistakes hide.

## Flashcards

### 5.7 | Expand $-3(2x - 5)$.
$-6x + 15$. The outside $-3$ times $-5$ gives $+15$.

### 5.7 | Expand $(x + 2)(x + 6)$.
$x^2 + 8x + 12$.

### 5.7 | Expand $(x + 3)^2$.
$x^2 + 6x + 9$ — not $x^2 + 9$.

### 5.7 | Factorise $12x - 8xy$ completely.
$4x(3 - 2y)$. The HCF is $4x$.

### 5.13 | Factorise $x^2 - 49$.
$(x + 7)(x - 7)$, the difference of two squares.

### 5.13 | Factorise $x^2 + 5x + 6$.
$(x + 2)(x + 3)$. The numbers 2 and 3 multiply to 6 and add to 5.

> Hint: multiply to $c$, add to $b$.

## Questions

### 5.7 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Expand $4(3x - 2)$.

- A. $12x - 2$
- B. $12x - 8$
- C. $7x - 6$
- D. $12x + 8$

**Answer:** B

**Working:** $4 \times 3x = 12x$ and $4 \times (-2) = -8$.

A only multiplies the first term.

### 5.7 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
$(x - 3)(x + 7)$ expands to

- A. $x^2 - 21$
- B. $x^2 + 4x - 21$
- C. $x^2 - 4x - 21$
- D. $x^2 + 10x - 21$

**Answer:** B

**Working:** $x^2 + 7x - 3x - 21 = x^2 + 4x - 21$.

A leaves out the middle terms. C gets the sign of the middle term wrong.

### 5.7 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Factorise completely: $6a^2 + 15a$.

- A. $3(2a^2 + 5a)$
- B. $a(6a + 15)$
- C. $3a(2a + 5)$
- D. $6a(a + 15)$

**Answer:** C

**Working:** The HCF of $6a^2$ and $15a$ is $3a$. Dividing out:
$6a^2 \div 3a = 2a$ and $15a \div 3a = 5$.

A and B are correct but not complete. D doesn't expand back to the original.

### 5.13 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Factorise $x^2 - x - 12$.

- A. $(x - 4)(x + 3)$
- B. $(x + 4)(x - 3)$
- C. $(x - 6)(x + 2)$
- D. $(x - 12)(x + 1)$

**Answer:** A

**Working:** Need two numbers that multiply to $-12$ and add to $-1$: $-4$ and
$+3$. So $(x - 4)(x + 3)$.

Check: $x^2 + 3x - 4x - 12 = x^2 - x - 12$. ✓ B gives $+x$ in the middle.

### 5.13 | structured | 6 marks | difficulty 3
Factorise completely:

(a) $2xy + 6y$ **(1 mark)**

(b) $16m^2 - 9$ **(2 marks)**

(c) $pq + 2p + 5q + 10$ **(2 marks)**

(d) $x^2 + 9x + 20$ **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) $2y(x + 3)$ (b) $(4m + 3)(4m - 3)$ (c) $(q + 2)(p + 5)$
(d) $(x + 4)(x + 5)$

**Working:** (a) HCF $2y$.

(b) $16m^2 = (4m)^2$ and $9 = 3^2$: a difference of two squares.

(c) $p(q + 2) + 5(q + 2) = (q + 2)(p + 5)$.

(d) 4 and 5 multiply to 20 and add to 9.
