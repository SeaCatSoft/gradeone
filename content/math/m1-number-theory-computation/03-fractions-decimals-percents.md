## Lesson

Fractions, decimals and percentages are three ways of writing the same
thing. $\frac{1}{2}$, $0.5$ and $50\%$ are one number in three outfits. Being
able to switch between them quickly is what lets you compare values and
answer "what fraction" and "what percentage" questions.

### Percent means "out of 100"

$37\%$ is $\frac{37}{100}$. Everything else follows from that.

| Change | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Percentage → fraction | put over 100, simplify | $35\% = \frac{35}{100} = \frac{7}{20}$ |
| Percentage → decimal | divide by 100 | $35\% = 0.35$ |
| Decimal → percentage | multiply by 100 | $0.08 = 8\%$ |
| Decimal → fraction | use place value, simplify | $0.125 = \frac{125}{1000} = \frac{1}{8}$ |
| Fraction → decimal | divide top by bottom | $\frac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8 = 0.375$ |
| Fraction → percentage | multiply by 100% | $\frac{3}{8} \times 100\% = 37.5\%$ |

Watch small percentages: $8\%$ is $0.08$, not $0.8$. And $0.8$ is $80\%$.

Some are worth knowing by heart, because they come up constantly:

| Fraction | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{4}$ | $\frac{3}{4}$ | $\frac{1}{5}$ | $\frac{1}{8}$ | $\frac{1}{10}$ | $\frac{1}{3}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decimal | 0.5 | 0.25 | 0.75 | 0.2 | 0.125 | 0.1 | $0.\dot{3}$ |
| Percentage | 50% | 25% | 75% | 20% | 12.5% | 10% | $33\frac{1}{3}\%$ |

### Finding a fraction or percentage of a quantity

"Of" means multiply.

$$\frac{3}{5} \text{ of } \$240 = \frac{3}{5} \times 240 = \frac{720}{5} = \$144$$

A neat way to do it in your head: divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.
$240 \div 5 = 48$, and $48 \times 3 = 144$.

For a percentage, change it to a fraction or decimal first:

$$15\% \text{ of } 360 = 0.15 \times 360 = 54$$

Or build it from easy pieces: $10\%$ of 360 is 36, $5\%$ is half of that,
18, so $15\% = 36 + 18 = 54$.

### Expressing one quantity as a fraction or percentage of another

Write it as a fraction — **the part over the whole** — then simplify or
convert:

> A class has 32 students and 12 of them walk to school. What percentage
> walk?

$$\frac{12}{32} \times 100\% = 37.5\%$$

The quantities must be in the **same units** first. To write 45 cents as a
percentage of \$3, change \$3 to 300 cents:

$$\frac{45}{300} \times 100\% = 15\%$$

Getting part and whole the right way round matters. "12 as a fraction of 32"
is $\frac{12}{32}$, never $\frac{32}{12}$.

### Comparing quantities

To decide which of two quantities is bigger, put them in the **same form**
and the **same units**, then compare. The symbols:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $<$ | is less than |
| $>$ | is greater than |
| $\leq$ | is less than or equal to |
| $\geq$ | is greater than or equal to |

The narrow end points at the smaller number: $3 < 5$.

Which is the better mark: 17 out of 20, or 42 out of 50?

$$\frac{17}{20} = 85\% \qquad \frac{42}{50} = 84\%$$

So $\frac{17}{20} > \frac{42}{50}$, and 17 out of 20 is the better mark — even
though 42 is the bigger number of marks. Comparing raw numbers from different
totals is the trap; converting to percentages removes it.

## Flashcards

### 1.4 | How do you change a percentage to a decimal?
Divide by 100. $35\% = 0.35$, and $8\% = 0.08$.

### 1.4 | How do you change a fraction to a decimal?
Divide the top by the bottom. $\frac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8 = 0.375$.

### 1.4 | Write $\frac{1}{8}$ as a decimal and as a percentage.
$0.125$ and $12.5\%$.

### 1.11 | Find $\frac{3}{4}$ of 60.
**45.** Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top: $60 \div 4 = 15$, and
$15 \times 3 = 45$.

### 1.11 | What does "of" mean in "20% of 150"?
Multiply. $20\% \text{ of } 150 = 0.2 \times 150 = 30$.

### 1.13 | How do you write one quantity as a percentage of another?
Put the part over the whole, in the **same units**, and multiply by 100%.

$$\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100\%$$

### 1.14 | Which is greater: $\frac{5}{8}$ or $0.6$?
$\frac{5}{8} = 0.625$, and $0.625 > 0.6$, so $\frac{5}{8}$ is greater.

## Questions

### 1.4 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
$0.07$ written as a percentage is

- A. $0.07\%$
- B. $0.7\%$
- C. $7\%$
- D. $70\%$

**Answer:** C

**Working:** Multiply by 100: $0.07 \times 100 = 7$, so $0.07 = 7\%$.

D comes from misreading $0.07$ as $0.7$.

### 1.4 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Write $\frac{7}{20}$ as a percentage. (Give the number only.)

**Answer:** 35

**Working:** $\frac{7}{20} \times 100\% = \frac{700}{20}\% = 35\%$.

Or: $\frac{7}{20} = \frac{35}{100}$, which is $35\%$.

### 1.11 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Calculate $\frac{2}{5}$ of \$85.

**Answer:** 34

**Working:** $85 \div 5 = 17$, and $17 \times 2 = 34$. The answer is \$34.

### 1.13 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Express 36 minutes as a percentage of 2 hours.

**Answer:** 30

**Working:** Change to the same units: 2 hours $= 120$ minutes.

$$\frac{36}{120} \times 100\% = 30\%$$

Using 2 as the whole gives $1800\%$, which should warn you something has
gone wrong.

### 1.14 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Which of these statements is **true**?

- A. $0.45 > \frac{1}{2}$
- B. $\frac{3}{5} < 55\%$
- C. $\frac{7}{8} > 0.8$
- D. $30\% = \frac{1}{3}$

**Answer:** C

**Working:** $\frac{7}{8} = 0.875$, which is greater than $0.8$. True.

A: $0.45 < 0.5$. B: $\frac{3}{5} = 60\%$, which is more than $55\%$. D: $30\% = 0.3$
but $\frac{1}{3} = 0.333\ldots$ — close, but not equal.

### 1.13 | structured | 4 marks | difficulty 3
A shop had 240 phones in stock. It sold 45% of them in January and $\frac{1}{3}$
of the **remaining** phones in February.

(a) How many phones were sold in January? **(1 mark)**

(b) How many phones were sold in February? **(2 marks)**

(c) What fraction of the original stock was left at the end of February?
Give your answer in its simplest form. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) 108 (b) 44 (c) $\frac{11}{30}$

**Working:** (a) $45\% \text{ of } 240 = 0.45 \times 240 = 108$.

(b) Remaining after January: $240 - 108 = 132$. February sales:
$\frac{1}{3} \times 132 = 44$. (1 mark for 132, 1 mark for 44.)

(c) Left: $132 - 44 = 88$. As a fraction of the original stock:
$\frac{88}{240} = \frac{11}{30}$.

The trap is in (b): $\frac{1}{3}$ of the *remaining* phones, not of 240.
