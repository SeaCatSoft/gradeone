## Lesson

Every non-vertical straight line has an equation of the form

$$y = mx + c$$

where $m$ is the gradient and $c$ the $y$-intercept. Finding a line's equation
means finding $m$ and $c$.

### Given the gradient and $y$-intercept

Write them straight in. Gradient 4, $y$-intercept $-3$: $y = 4x - 3$.

### Given the gradient and one point

Substitute the point into $y = mx + c$ to find $c$.

> Find the equation of the line with gradient 2 through $(3, 11)$.

$$11 = 2(3) + c \quad\Rightarrow\quad c = 5$$

The equation is $y = 2x + 5$.

An equivalent method uses the **point-gradient form**:

$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

$y - 11 = 2(x - 3)$, so $y = 2x - 6 + 11 = 2x + 5$. Same line.

### Given two points

Find the gradient first, then use either point.

> Find the equation of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 14)$.

$$m = \frac{14 - 2}{5 - 1} = 3 \qquad 2 = 3(1) + c \;\Rightarrow\; c = -1$$

The equation is $y = 3x - 1$.

**Check** with the other point: $3(5) - 1 = 14$. ✓

### Other forms

An answer may be asked for in the form $ax + by = c$ or $ax + by + c = 0$,
usually with whole numbers. From $y = \frac{1}{2}x + 3$, multiply by 2:
$2y = x + 6$, so $x - 2y + 6 = 0$.

### Vertical and horizontal lines

- A horizontal line through $(4, -2)$: $y = -2$.
- A vertical line through $(4, -2)$: $x = 4$. (It has no $y = mx + c$ form.)

## Flashcards

### 3.7 | What do $m$ and $c$ stand for in $y = mx + c$?
$m$ is the gradient; $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

### 3.7 | How do you find $c$ when you know $m$ and one point?
Substitute the point's coordinates into $y = mx + c$ and solve for $c$.

### 3.7 | State the point-gradient form of a line.
$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

### 3.7 | What is the equation of the vertical line through $(5, 2)$?
$x = 5$.

### 3.7 | Line through $(0, 4)$ with gradient $-3$?
$y = -3x + 4$.

## Questions

### 3.7 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which is the equation of the line with gradient 5 and $y$-intercept $-2$?

- A. $y = -2x + 5$
- B. $y = 5x - 2$
- C. $y = 5x + 2$
- D. $y = 2x - 5$

**Answer:** B

**Working:** $y = mx + c$ with $m = 5$ and $c = -2$.

### 3.7 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A line has gradient $-2$ and passes through $(4, 1)$. Its equation is
$y = -2x + c$. Find $c$.

**Answer:** 9

**Working:** $1 = -2(4) + c$, so $c = 9$.

### 3.7 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Find the equation of the line through $(-1, 7)$ and $(2, -2)$.

- A. $y = -3x + 4$
- B. $y = 3x + 10$
- C. $y = -3x + 7$
- D. $y = -\frac{1}{3}x + 4$

**Answer:** A

**Working:** $m = \dfrac{-2 - 7}{2 - (-1)} = \dfrac{-9}{3} = -3$.
$7 = -3(-1) + c$, so $c = 4$. Equation: $y = -3x + 4$.

Check with $(2, -2)$: $-6 + 4 = -2$. ✓

### 3.7 | structured | 4 marks | difficulty 3
The line $L$ passes through $P(2, 3)$ and $Q(6, 5)$.

(a) Find the gradient of $L$. **(1 mark)**

(b) Find the equation of $L$ in the form $y = mx + c$. **(2 marks)**

(c) Write the equation of $L$ in the form $ax + by + c = 0$, where $a$, $b$ and
$c$ are integers. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) $\frac{1}{2}$ (b) $y = \frac{1}{2}x + 2$ (c) $x - 2y + 4 = 0$

**Working:** (a) $\frac{5 - 3}{6 - 2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$.

(b) $3 = \frac{1}{2}(2) + c$, so $c = 2$.

(c) Multiply by 2: $2y = x + 4$, so $x - 2y + 4 = 0$.
