## Lesson

Graphs turn a relationship into a picture you can read. In problems, the key
questions are always the same: **what do the axes measure, what does the
gradient mean, and what does the area mean?**

### Distance–time graphs

Distance from a starting point goes up the side; time goes along the bottom.

- The **gradient** is the **speed**: distance ÷ time.
- A **horizontal** section means the object is **stationary** (distance isn't
  changing).
- A **steeper** line means a **faster** speed.
- A line sloping **down** means the object is coming **back** towards the start.

> A cyclist travels 12 km in the first 40 minutes, stops for 20 minutes, then
> rides home in 30 minutes.

Speed on the way out: $12 \div \frac{40}{60} = 18$ km/h. Speed home:
$12 \div \frac{30}{60} = 24$ km/h — the return section is steeper.

A **curved** distance–time graph means the speed is changing: getting steeper
means speeding up.

### Speed–time graphs

Speed goes up the side, time along the bottom.

- The **gradient** is the **acceleration**: change in speed ÷ time.
- A **horizontal** section means **constant speed** (not stationary!).
- The **area under the graph** is the **distance travelled**.

> A car accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s, holds 20 m/s for 30 s, then
> slows to rest in 5 s.

- Acceleration: $\frac{20}{10} = 2$ m/s²
- Distance = area = triangle + rectangle + triangle
  $= \frac{1}{2}(10)(20) + 30(20) + \frac{1}{2}(5)(20) = 100 + 600 + 50 = 750$ m

Mixing up the two graph types is the classic error. On a distance–time graph, a
flat line means stopped; on a speed–time graph, it means moving steadily.

### Other real-world graphs

For any graph from a context:

- Read the **units** on each axis before anything else.
- The **gradient** of a straight section is a **rate** (units of $y$ per unit of
  $x$): litres per minute, dollars per hour.
- A **maximum** or **minimum** point is usually the answer to "greatest" or
  "least" questions.

## Questions

### 3.20 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
On a distance–time graph, a car travels 150 km in the first 2 hours. What is
its speed, in km/h?

**Answer:** 75

**Working:** Gradient $= 150 \div 2 = 75$ km/h.

### 3.20 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
On a speed–time graph, a straight line rises from 4 m/s to 16 m/s over 6
seconds. What is the acceleration?

- A. 2 m/s²
- B. 2.67 m/s²
- C. 12 m/s²
- D. 72 m/s²

**Answer:** A

**Working:** Change in speed $= 16 - 4 = 12$ m/s over 6 s: $12 \div 6 = 2$ m/s².

B uses $16 \div 6$, ignoring the starting speed.

### 3.20 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A train accelerates uniformly from rest to 30 m/s in 20 s, then travels at
30 m/s for 60 s. Using the area under its speed–time graph, find the total
distance travelled, in metres.

**Answer:** 2100

**Working:** Triangle: $\frac{1}{2} \times 20 \times 30 = 300$ m. Rectangle:
$60 \times 30 = 1800$ m. Total: $2100$ m.

### 3.20 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 3
Ann walks from home to a shop 1.2 km away in 15 minutes, spends 10 minutes in
the shop, then walks home in 20 minutes.

(a) Describe the section of her distance–time graph while she is in the shop.
**(1 mark)**

(b) Calculate her speed on the way to the shop, in km/h. **(2 marks)**

(c) Was she faster on the way to the shop or on the way home? Explain using the
graph. **(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) A horizontal line at 1.2 km, lasting 10 minutes (b) 4.8 km/h
(c) On the way to the shop: that section of the graph is steeper.

**Working:** (b) 15 minutes $= 0.25$ h (1 mark). $1.2 \div 0.25 = 4.8$ km/h
(1 mark).

(c) Home: $1.2 \div \frac{20}{60} = 3.6$ km/h, which is slower. The outward section
covers the same distance in less time, so it's steeper (1 mark for the choice,
1 for the reason).
