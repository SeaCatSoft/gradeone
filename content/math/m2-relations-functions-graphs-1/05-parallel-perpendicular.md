## Lesson

### Parallel lines

**Parallel** lines never meet, because they have the same steepness:

> Parallel lines have **equal gradients**.

$y = 3x + 1$ and $y = 3x - 7$ are parallel (both gradient 3). They differ only
in where they cross the $y$-axis.

### Perpendicular lines

**Perpendicular** lines meet at a right angle. Their gradients are linked:

> If two lines are perpendicular, $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$.

So each gradient is the **negative reciprocal** of the other: flip the fraction
and change the sign.

| Gradient | Perpendicular gradient |
|---|---|
| $2$ | $-\frac{1}{2}$ |
| $-\frac{3}{4}$ | $\frac{4}{3}$ |
| $\frac{1}{5}$ | $-5$ |
| $-1$ | $1$ |

Check: $2 \times -\frac{1}{2} = -1$. ✓

(A horizontal line, gradient 0, is perpendicular to a vertical line, whose
gradient is undefined. The rule doesn't apply to that pair.)

### Finding the equation of a parallel or perpendicular line

1. Find the gradient of the given line (rearrange to $y = mx + c$ if needed).
2. Use the **same** gradient for a parallel line, or the **negative reciprocal**
   for a perpendicular one.
3. Substitute the given point to find $c$.

> Find the equation of the line through $(4, 1)$ perpendicular to $y = 2x - 3$.

The given gradient is 2, so the perpendicular gradient is $-\frac{1}{2}$.

$$1 = -\tfrac{1}{2}(4) + c \quad\Rightarrow\quad c = 3$$

The equation is $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 3$.

### Showing lines are perpendicular

Find both gradients and multiply. If the product is $-1$, they're
perpendicular. This is how you prove a triangle has a right angle, using the
gradients of two of its sides.

## Flashcards

### 3.8 | What is true about the gradients of parallel lines?
They are **equal**.

### 3.8 | What is true about the gradients of perpendicular lines?
They multiply to $-1$: each is the negative reciprocal of the other.

### 3.8 | What gradient is perpendicular to a gradient of $\frac{2}{3}$?
$-\frac{3}{2}$.

### 3.8 | Are $y = 4x + 1$ and $y = -\frac{1}{4}x + 6$ perpendicular?
Yes. $4 \times -\frac{1}{4} = -1$.

### 3.8 | Line parallel to $y = 5x - 2$ through the origin?
$y = 5x$.

## Questions

### 3.8 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which line is parallel to $y = -2x + 5$?

- A. $y = 2x + 5$
- B. $y = -2x - 3$
- C. $y = \frac{1}{2}x + 5$
- D. $y = 5x - 2$

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Parallel lines have the same gradient, $-2$.

### 3.8 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
What is the gradient of a line perpendicular to $3x + y = 4$?

- A. $-3$
- B. $3$
- C. $\frac{1}{3}$
- D. $-\frac{1}{3}$

**Answer:** C

**Working:** $y = -3x + 4$ has gradient $-3$. The negative reciprocal of $-3$ is
$\frac{1}{3}$.

### 3.8 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
The line through $(2, 5)$ perpendicular to $y = \frac{1}{3}x + 1$ has equation
$y = mx + c$. Find $c$.

**Answer:** 11

**Working:** The perpendicular gradient is $-3$. $5 = -3(2) + c$, so $c = 11$.

### 3.8 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 4
The points are $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 3)$ and $C(2, 6)$.

(a) Find the gradient of $AB$ and the gradient of $BC$. **(2 marks)**

(b) Hence show that angle $ABC$ is a right angle. **(1 mark)**

(c) Find the equation of the line through $C$ parallel to $AB$. **(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) $AB$: $\frac{2}{3}$; $BC$: $-\frac{3}{2}$ (b) See working
(c) $y = \frac{2}{3}x + \frac{14}{3}$

**Working:** (a) $AB$: $\frac{3 - 1}{4 - 1} = \frac{2}{3}$. $BC$:
$\frac{6 - 3}{2 - 4} = -\frac{3}{2}$.

(b) $\frac{2}{3} \times -\frac{3}{2} = -1$, so $AB \perp BC$ and angle $ABC = 90°$.

(c) Gradient $\frac{2}{3}$; $6 = \frac{2}{3}(2) + c$, so $c = 6 - \frac{4}{3} = \frac{14}{3}$.
(Equivalently, $3y = 2x + 14$.)
