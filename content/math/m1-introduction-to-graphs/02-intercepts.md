## Lesson

The **intercepts** of a line are where it crosses the axes.

- The **$y$-intercept** is where it crosses the $y$-axis. There, $x = 0$.
- The **$x$-intercept** is where it crosses the $x$-axis. There, $y = 0$.

That one fact — **on an axis, the other coordinate is zero** — gives both
methods.

### Finding intercepts from a graph

Read off where the line meets each axis.

The line $y = 6 - 2x$ above crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, 6)$ and the $x$-axis at
$(3, 0)$.

Give an intercept either as a point, $(3, 0)$, or as a value, "the
$x$-intercept is 3" — whichever the question asks for.

### Finding intercepts algebraically

Substitute zero for the **other** letter.

For $y = 6 - 2x$:

- **$y$-intercept:** put $x = 0$. $y = 6 - 0 = 6$, giving $(0, 6)$.
- **$x$-intercept:** put $y = 0$. $0 = 6 - 2x$, so $2x = 6$ and $x = 3$, giving
  $(3, 0)$.

This matches the graph. Algebra gives exact values even when the crossing
point falls between grid lines.

In the form $y = mx + c$, the $y$-intercept is simply $c$. For $y = 4x - 7$ it's
$-7$ — no working needed.

### Equations in the form $ax + by = c$

Intercepts make lines like $3x + 4y = 12$ quick to draw:

- $x = 0$: $4y = 12$, so $y = 3$. Point $(0, 3)$.
- $y = 0$: $3x = 12$, so $x = 4$. Point $(4, 0)$.

Plot both intercepts and rule the line through them. (A third point, such as
$x = 8$, $y = -3$, is a good check.)

### A line through the origin

$y = 5x$ has $c = 0$, so both intercepts are at $(0, 0)$. It passes through the
origin, and you'll need a second point, such as $(1, 5)$, to draw it.

## Flashcards

### 6.2 | At the $y$-intercept, what is the value of $x$?
$x = 0$.

### 6.2 | At the $x$-intercept, what is the value of $y$?
$y = 0$.

### 6.2 | What is the $y$-intercept of $y = 3x + 8$?
8. In $y = mx + c$, the $y$-intercept is $c$.

### 6.2 | Find the $x$-intercept of $y = 2x - 10$.
5. Put $y = 0$: $2x - 10 = 0$, so $x = 5$.

> Hint: which coordinate is zero on the $x$-axis?

### 6.2 | Find both intercepts of $2x + 5y = 20$.
$(0, 4)$ and $(10, 0)$.

## Questions

### 6.2 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
State the $y$-intercept of the line $y = 7 - 3x$.

**Answer:** 7

**Working:** Put $x = 0$: $y = 7 - 0 = 7$.

### 6.2 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Find the $x$-intercept of the line $y = 4x + 12$.

**Answer:** -3

**Working:** Put $y = 0$: $4x + 12 = 0$, so $4x = -12$ and $x = -3$.

### 6.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Where does the line $5x + 2y = 10$ cross the $x$-axis?

- A. $(0, 5)$
- B. $(2, 0)$
- C. $(5, 0)$
- D. $(0, 2)$

**Answer:** B

**Working:** On the $x$-axis, $y = 0$: $5x = 10$, so $x = 2$. The point is
$(2, 0)$.

A is the $y$-intercept. C and D mix up which number goes with which letter.

### 6.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A line has $y$-intercept $-4$ and passes through $(2, 0)$. Its equation is

- A. $y = 2x - 4$
- B. $y = -2x - 4$
- C. $y = 4x - 2$
- D. $y = -4x + 2$

**Answer:** A

**Working:** The $y$-intercept is $-4$, so $c = -4$: $y = mx - 4$. At $(2, 0)$:
$0 = 2m - 4$, so $m = 2$. The equation is $y = 2x - 4$.

Check B: at $x = 2$, $y = -8$, not 0.

### 6.2 | structured | 4 marks | difficulty 3
The line $L$ has equation $3x + 2y = 18$.

(a) Find the coordinates of the point where $L$ crosses the $y$-axis.
**(1 mark)**

(b) Find the coordinates of the point where $L$ crosses the $x$-axis.
**(1 mark)**

(c) Does the point $(4, 3)$ lie on $L$? Show how you decide. **(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) $(0, 9)$ (b) $(6, 0)$ (c) Yes

**Working:** (a) $x = 0$: $2y = 18$, so $y = 9$.

(b) $y = 0$: $3x = 18$, so $x = 6$.

(c) Substitute: $3(4) + 2(3) = 12 + 6 = 18$ (1 mark). The equation is
satisfied, so $(4, 3)$ lies on $L$ (1 mark).
