## Lesson

Paper 02 measurement questions usually combine several ideas: a volume that
must become litres, an area that decides how much paint to buy, a
circumference that tells you how far a wheel rolls. This lesson collects the
habits that make those questions go smoothly.

### 1. Sketch and label

Even if a diagram is given, sketch the shape you're working on and write every
measurement on it, in one unit. Many errors come from using a diameter as a
radius, or mixing cm with m.

### 2. Name the quantity

Ask what the question wants:

| Words in the question | Quantity | Units |
|---|---|---|
| fence, border, edging, trim, distance round | perimeter / circumference | m, cm |
| paint, tiles, carpet, grass, cover | area / surface area | m², cm² |
| fill, hold, capacity, how much water | volume | m³, cm³, L |
| how far, how fast, how long | speed, distance, time | km/h, km, h |

### 3. Convert once, early

Change all measurements to the same unit **before** calculating, and convert
the answer at the end if it's needed in another unit. Converting halfway
through is how factors of 100 go missing.

### 4. Round sensibly at the end

Real-world answers often need rounding in a particular direction:

- Tins, bags, tiles, trips: round **up** — 8.2 tins means you must buy 9.
- Things that fit, complete laps, full containers you can fill: round
  **down** — you can only fill 12 whole bottles from 12.8 bottles' worth.

### Worked example

> A cylindrical water tank has diameter 1.4 m and height 2 m. It is filled by
> a hose delivering 20 litres per minute. How long does it take to fill?
> Use $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.

- Radius: $1.4 \div 2 = 0.7$ m
- Volume: $\frac{22}{7} \times 0.7^2 \times 2 = 1.54 \times 2 = 3.08$ m³
- In litres: $3.08 \times 1000 = 3080$ L
- Time: $3080 \div 20 = 154$ minutes $= 2$ h 34 min

Every step is a skill from an earlier lesson. The only new thing is linking
them.

## Flashcards

### 4.13 | A question asks how much fencing is needed. Which quantity?
**Perimeter.**

### 4.13 | A question asks how many tins of paint to buy. Which quantity, and which way do you round?
**Area**, and round **up** — you can't buy part of a tin.

### 4.13 | When should you convert units in a multi-step problem?
**Early**, before calculating. Put every measurement in the same unit first.

### 4.13 | A wheel of radius 35 cm makes one full turn. How far does it roll? Use $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.
Its circumference: $2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 35 = 220$ cm.

## Questions

### 4.13 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A bicycle wheel has a diameter of 70 cm. How many complete turns does it make
in travelling 440 m? Use $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.

**Answer:** 200

**Working:** Circumference: $\frac{22}{7} \times 70 = 220$ cm $= 2.2$ m.
Turns: $440 \div 2.2 = 200$.

### 4.13 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A rectangular swimming pool is 25 m long, 10 m wide and 1.2 m deep. How many
litres of water does it hold when full?

- A. 300 L
- B. 3 000 L
- C. 30 000 L
- D. 300 000 L

**Answer:** D

**Working:** Volume $= 25 \times 10 \times 1.2 = 300$ m³.
$300 \times 1000 = 300\,000$ L.

A leaves the answer in cubic metres.

### 4.13 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A room's floor is 5.4 m by 3.6 m. It is to be covered with square tiles of side
30 cm. How many tiles are needed?

**Answer:** 216

**Working:** Convert to cm: 540 cm by 360 cm.
Tiles along each side: $540 \div 30 = 18$ and $360 \div 30 = 12$.
Total: $18 \times 12 = 216$ tiles.

Or by area: $\dfrac{540 \times 360}{30 \times 30} = \dfrac{194\,400}{900} = 216$.
Dividing 5.4 × 3.6 = 19.44 m² by 30 × 30 = 900 mixes m² with cm² — convert first.

### 4.13 | structured | 6 marks | difficulty 4
A garden is a rectangle 20 m by 14 m. A circular pond of radius 3.5 m is dug
in the middle, and the rest of the garden is to be grassed. A path is laid all
the way around the **outside** edge of the garden. Use $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.

(a) Find the area of the pond. **(1 mark)**

(b) Find the area to be grassed. **(2 marks)**

(c) Grass seed is sold in boxes that cover 25 m². How many boxes are needed?
**(1 mark)**

(d) Edging for the path costs \$12.50 per metre. Find the cost of edging the
outside of the garden. **(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) 38.5 m² (b) 241.5 m² (c) 10 boxes (d) \$850

**Working:** (a) $\frac{22}{7} \times 3.5^2 = \frac{22}{7} \times 12.25 = 38.5$ m².

(b) Garden: $20 \times 14 = 280$ m² (1 mark). Grass: $280 - 38.5 = 241.5$ m²
(1 mark).

(c) $241.5 \div 25 = 9.66$, so **10** boxes (round up).

(d) Perimeter: $2(20) + 2(14) = 68$ m (1 mark). Cost:
$68 \times 12.50 = 850$ (1 mark).
