## Lesson

### Choosing the right unit

The **SI** system (the international metric system) gives each kind of
quantity its own units:

| Quantity | Common SI units |
|---|---|
| Length | mm, cm, m, km |
| Mass | mg, g, kg, tonne (t) |
| Area | mm², cm², m², km², hectare (ha) |
| Volume | mm³, cm³, m³ |
| Capacity | mL, L |
| Time | s, min, h |
| Temperature | °C |

Pick a unit that suits the size of the thing. A pencil is measured in
centimetres, a road in kilometres; a tablet in milligrams, a truck's load in
tonnes.

Area units are **squared** and volume units are **cubed**, because an area is
a length times a length, and a volume is three lengths multiplied.

Quantities built from other units are **derived**. Speed is distance ÷ time,
so its units are km/h or m/s. Density is mass ÷ volume, in g/cm³.

### The 24-hour clock

The 24-hour clock runs from 00:00 (midnight) to 23:59. Afternoon times add 12:

| 12-hour | 24-hour |
|---|---|
| 12:15 a.m. | 00:15 |
| 7:05 a.m. | 07:05 |
| 12:30 p.m. | 12:30 |
| 4:45 p.m. | 16:45 |
| 11:20 p.m. | 23:20 |

To find a time difference, count up to the next whole hour, then on. From
09:40 to 13:15 is 20 minutes to 10:00, three hours to 13:00, and 15 minutes
more: **3 h 35 min**.

### Converting area units — the trap

$1 \text{ m} = 100 \text{ cm}$. So is $1 \text{ m}^2 = 100 \text{ cm}^2$?

**No.** A square metre is a square 100 cm long **and** 100 cm wide:

$$1 \text{ m}^2 = 100 \text{ cm} \times 100 \text{ cm} = 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2$$

For area, **square** the length conversion factor:

| Length | Area |
|---|---|
| 1 cm = 10 mm | 1 cm² = $10^2$ = 100 mm² |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 m² = $100^2$ = 10 000 cm² |
| 1 km = 1000 m | 1 km² = $1000^2$ = 1 000 000 m² |

Land is often measured in **hectares**: 1 ha = 10 000 m² (a square 100 m by
100 m).

### Converting volume units

For volume, **cube** the length conversion factor:

$$1 \text{ m}^3 = 100^3 \text{ cm}^3 = 1\,000\,000 \text{ cm}^3$$

$$1 \text{ cm}^3 = 10^3 \text{ mm}^3 = 1000 \text{ mm}^3$$

### Volume and capacity

**Capacity** is how much a container holds, usually of a liquid. The link to
volume:

$$1 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ mL} \qquad 1000 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ L} \qquad 1 \text{ m}^3 = 1000 \text{ L}$$

So a tank of volume $2 \text{ m}^3$ holds $2000$ litres.

## Flashcards

### 4.1 | How many cm² are in 1 m²?
$$10\,000 \text{ cm}^2$$

$1 \text{ m}^2 = 100 \text{ cm} \times 100 \text{ cm}$. Square the length factor.

> Hint: it's not 100.

### 4.1 | How many cm³ are in 1 m³?
$$1\,000\,000 \text{ cm}^3$$

Cube the length factor: $100^3$.

### 4.1 | How many millilitres is 1 cm³?
Exactly 1 mL. So $1000 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ L}$.

### 4.1 | How many litres does 1 m³ hold?
1000 litres.

### 4.2 | Write 6:50 p.m. in 24-hour time.
18:50. Add 12 to the hours for times after noon.

### 4.1 | How many square metres is one hectare?
10 000 m² — a square 100 m by 100 m.

## Questions

### 4.1 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Convert 3.5 m² to cm².

**Answer:** 35000

**Working:** $1 \text{ m}^2 = 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2$, so
$3.5 \times 10\,000 = 35\,000 \text{ cm}^2$.

Multiplying by 100 instead gives 350, the most common wrong answer.

### 4.1 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A tank has a volume of $0.8 \text{ m}^3$. How many litres does it hold?

- A. 0.8 L
- B. 8 L
- C. 80 L
- D. 800 L

**Answer:** D

**Working:** $1 \text{ m}^3 = 1000 \text{ L}$, so $0.8 \times 1000 = 800 \text{ L}$.

### 4.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which unit is most suitable for the mass of a bag of sugar?

- A. mg
- B. kg
- C. tonnes
- D. km

**Answer:** B

**Working:** A bag of sugar is about 1 or 2 kg. Milligrams would give an
enormous number, tonnes a tiny one, and km measures length, not mass.

### 4.2 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A bus leaves at 10:45 and arrives at 14:20 the same day. How long is the
journey, in minutes?

**Answer:** 215

**Working:** 10:45 to 11:00 is 15 min. 11:00 to 14:00 is 3 h = 180 min. 14:00 to
14:20 is 20 min. Total: $15 + 180 + 20 = 215$ minutes.

### 4.1 | structured | 4 marks | difficulty 3
A rectangular fish tank measures 80 cm by 40 cm by 50 cm.

(a) Calculate its volume in cm³. **(1 mark)**

(b) Express this volume in m³. **(1 mark)**

(c) How many litres of water does the tank hold when full? **(1 mark)**

(d) A jug holds 750 mL. How many full jugs are needed to fill the tank?
**(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) 160 000 cm³ (b) 0.16 m³ (c) 160 L (d) 214 jugs

**Working:** (a) $80 \times 40 \times 50 = 160\,000 \text{ cm}^3$.

(b) $160\,000 \div 1\,000\,000 = 0.16 \text{ m}^3$.

(c) $1000 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ L}$, so $160\,000 \div 1000 = 160 \text{ L}$.

(d) $160\,000 \text{ mL} \div 750 \text{ mL} = 213.3\ldots$ Since 213 jugs would
leave the tank a little short, **214** jugs are needed.
