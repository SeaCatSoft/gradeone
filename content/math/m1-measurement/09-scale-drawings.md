## Lesson

Maps and plans are drawn smaller than real life, but in proportion. The
**scale** says how much smaller.

### Two ways to write a scale

**As a statement:** "1 cm represents 5 m". Every centimetre on the drawing
stands for 5 metres in real life.

**As a ratio:** $1 : 500$. One unit on the drawing is 500 of the **same** units
in real life. The ratio has no units, because they cancel.

The two can describe the same scale. 1 cm represents 5 m, and
$5 \text{ m} = 500 \text{ cm}$, so the ratio is $1 : 500$.

### Drawing to real

**Multiply** by the scale, then convert to a sensible unit.

> On a map with scale $1 : 50\,000$, two villages are 6 cm apart. How far
> apart are they really?

$$6 \times 50\,000 = 300\,000 \text{ cm} = 3000 \text{ m} = 3 \text{ km}$$

Converting large numbers of centimetres: divide by 100 for metres, then by
1000 for kilometres. So divide by 100 000 to go straight from cm to km.

### Real to drawing

**Divide** by the scale.

> A 24 m wall is drawn on a plan with scale $1 : 200$. How long is the line?

Convert first: $24 \text{ m} = 2400 \text{ cm}$. Then $2400 \div 200 = 12$ cm.

### Areas on a scale drawing

Here's the trap. On a $1 : 500$ drawing, 1 cm represents 5 m — but **1 cm²
does not represent 5 m²**.

A 1 cm by 1 cm square on the drawing is a 5 m by 5 m square in real life, which
is $25$ m². Just as with converting area units, the area scale is the **square**
of the length scale:

$$1 \text{ cm}^2 \text{ on the drawing} \;\to\; 5^2 = 25 \text{ m}^2 \text{ in real life}$$

> A field on a $1 : 500$ plan has an area of 12 cm². What is its real area?

$$12 \times 25 = 300 \text{ m}^2$$

The safest method, if in doubt: convert the **lengths** to real lengths first,
then calculate the area.

## Flashcards

### 4.12 | What does a scale of $1 : 25\,000$ mean?
1 unit on the map represents 25 000 of the same units in real life. For
example, 1 cm represents 25 000 cm $= 250$ m.

### 4.12 | Write "1 cm represents 2 km" as a ratio.
$1 : 200\,000$. $2 \text{ km} = 200\,000 \text{ cm}$.

### 4.12 | Map distance to real distance: multiply or divide?
**Multiply** by the scale factor.

### 4.12 | 1 cm represents 10 m. What does 1 cm² represent?
100 m². The area scale is the **square** of the length scale.

> Hint: draw the 1 cm square and label its real sides.

## Questions

### 4.12 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
A plan has a scale of 1 cm to 4 m. A room is 3.5 cm long on the plan. How
long is the real room, in metres?

**Answer:** 14

**Working:** $3.5 \times 4 = 14$ m.

### 4.12 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
On a map with scale $1 : 25\,000$, a road measures 18 cm. Find the real length
of the road, in km.

**Answer:** 4.5

**Working:** $18 \times 25\,000 = 450\,000$ cm. Divide by 100 000 to get km:
$4.5$ km.

### 4.12 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A car park is 60 m long. On a plan with scale $1 : 400$, how long is it drawn?

- A. 0.15 cm
- B. 1.5 cm
- C. 15 cm
- D. 24 cm

**Answer:** C

**Working:** $60 \text{ m} = 6000 \text{ cm}$. $6000 \div 400 = 15$ cm.

A divides 60 by 400 without converting metres to centimetres.

### 4.12 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
On a plan where 1 cm represents 20 m, a park has an area of 15 cm². Find its
real area, in m².

**Answer:** 6000

**Working:** 1 cm² represents $20 \times 20 = 400$ m².
$15 \times 400 = 6000$ m².

Multiplying by 20 instead gives 300 m², twenty times too small.

### 4.12 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 3
A rectangular plot of land is drawn on a plan to a scale of $1 : 500$. On the
plan it measures 8 cm by 5 cm.

(a) Find the real length and width of the plot, in metres. **(2 marks)**

(b) Find the real area of the plot, in m². **(1 mark)**

(c) Land costs \$150 per square metre. Find the cost of the plot. **(1 mark)**

(d) Find the area of the plot in hectares. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) 40 m by 25 m (b) 1000 m² (c) \$150 000 (d) 0.1 ha

**Working:** (a) $8 \times 500 = 4000$ cm $= 40$ m;
$5 \times 500 = 2500$ cm $= 25$ m (1 mark each).

(b) $40 \times 25 = 1000$ m².

(c) $1000 \times 150 = 150\,000$, so \$150 000.

(d) $1 \text{ ha} = 10\,000 \text{ m}^2$, so $1000 \div 10\,000 = 0.1$ ha.
