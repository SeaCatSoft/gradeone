## Lesson

**Volume** is the amount of space a solid takes up, measured in cubic units:
cm³, m³ and so on.

### Cuboids

$$V = l \times w \times h$$

A box 12 cm by 5 cm by 4 cm holds $12 \times 5 \times 4 = 240$ cm³. You could
think of it as 4 layers, each of $12 \times 5 = 60$ unit cubes.

### Prisms: one rule for many shapes

That "layers" idea works for every prism. A prism has the same cross-section
all the way through, so:

$$V = \text{area of cross-section} \times \text{length}$$

This one formula covers cuboids, triangular prisms, cylinders, and any other
prism. Find the area of the end face (using the area formulas you already
know), then multiply by how long the prism is.

> A triangular prism has a triangular cross-section with base 6 cm and
> height 4 cm, and it is 15 cm long.

$$V = \left(\frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4\right) \times 15 = 12 \times 15 = 180 \text{ cm}^3$$

### Cylinders

A cylinder is a prism whose cross-section is a circle:

$$V = \pi r^2 h$$

> Find the volume of a cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 10 cm. Use
> $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.

$$V = \frac{22}{7} \times 7^2 \times 10 = 154 \times 10 = 1540 \text{ cm}^3$$

### Pointed solids and spheres

A **pyramid** or **cone** holds exactly **one third** of the prism or cylinder
with the same base and height:

$$V_{\text{pyramid}} = \frac{1}{3} \times \text{base area} \times h \qquad V_{\text{cone}} = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$$

A cone of radius 7 cm and height 12 cm: $\frac{1}{3} \times 154 \times 12 = 616$ cm³.

A **sphere** of radius $r$:

$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$

### Volume and capacity

Volume answers often need converting to litres at the end. Remember
$1000 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ L}$ and $1 \text{ m}^3 = 1000 \text{ L}$.

### Working backwards

If you know the volume and all but one measurement, rearrange. A cuboid tank
of volume 360 cm³ with a base 10 cm by 6 cm has height
$360 \div (10 \times 6) = 6$ cm.

## Flashcards

$$V = \pi r^2 h$$

### 4.9 | How does a cone's volume compare with a cylinder's with the same base and height?

### 4.9 | What is the volume of a sphere?
$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$

5 cm. $200 \div (8 \times 5) = 200 \div 40 = 5$.

## Questions

### 4.9 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Find the volume of a cuboid measuring 9 cm by 4 cm by 5 cm, in cm³.

**Answer:** 180

**Working:** $9 \times 4 \times 5 = 180$ cm³.

### 4.9 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A cylinder has radius 14 cm and height 5 cm. Find its volume, in cm³. Use
$\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.

**Answer:** 3080

**Working:** $\frac{22}{7} \times 14^2 \times 5 = 616 \times 5 = 3080$ cm³.

### 4.9 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A prism has a cross-section of area 24 cm² and a length of 11 cm. Its volume is

- A. 35 cm³
- B. 132 cm³
- C. 264 cm³
- D. 528 cm³

**Answer:** C

**Working:** $V = 24 \times 11 = 264$ cm³.

### 4.9 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A cone has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. Taking $\pi = 3.14$, find its volume,
in cm³.

**Answer:** 94.2

**Working:** $\frac{1}{3} \times 3.14 \times 3^2 \times 10 = \frac{1}{3} \times 282.6 = 94.2$ cm³.

### 4.9 | structured | 6 marks | difficulty 4
A cylindrical water tank has an internal radius of 0.7 m and a height of
1.5 m. Use $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.

(a) Calculate the volume of the tank, in m³. **(2 marks)**

(b) How many litres does it hold when full? **(1 mark)**

(c) Water is used at 45 litres per day. Starting full, for how many **complete**
days will the tank supply water? **(1 mark)**

(d) The tank is refilled by a pipe delivering 12 litres per minute. How long,
in hours and minutes, does it take to fill the empty tank? **(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) 2.31 m³ (b) 2310 L (c) 51 days (d) 3 h 12.5 min, which is
3 h 13 min to the nearest minute

**Working:** (a) $V = \frac{22}{7} \times 0.7^2 \times 1.5 = \frac{22}{7} \times 0.49 \times 1.5 = 1.54 \times 1.5 = 2.31$ m³.

(b) $2.31 \times 1000 = 2310$ L.

(c) $2310 \div 45 = 51.33\ldots$, so **51** complete days.

(d) $2310 \div 12 = 192.5$ minutes (1 mark). $192.5 = 180 + 12.5$, which is
3 hours 12.5 minutes (1 mark).
