## Lesson

### Histograms

A **histogram** displays grouped continuous data.

- The horizontal axis is a **continuous number line** of class boundaries.
- Bars touch, with **no gaps** — classes run on into each other.
- With **equal** class widths, the **height** of each bar is the frequency.

Choose a scale that uses most of the grid, label both axes with the quantity
**and its units**, and give the histogram a title.

A histogram is not a bar chart: bar charts show separate categories and have
gaps; histograms show a continuous scale and don't.

### Frequency polygons

A **frequency polygon** plots the frequency against the **class midpoint** and
joins the points with straight lines.

| Class | $0 \leq x < 10$ | $10 \leq x < 20$ | $20 \leq x < 30$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Midpoint | 5 | 15 | 25 |
| Frequency | 6 | 14 | 9 |

Plot $(5, 6)$, $(15, 14)$ and $(25, 9)$ and join them.

Note the difference from a cumulative frequency curve, which uses **upper
boundaries** and a **running total**. A frequency polygon uses **midpoints** and
the **plain** frequencies.

Frequency polygons are useful for **comparing** two sets of data: draw both on
the same axes, and the shapes can be compared directly.

### Line graphs

A **line graph** shows change over time — monthly rainfall, daily sales — with
time on the horizontal axis.

### Analysing a diagram

Questions ask you to read values and describe patterns:

- Identify the **modal class** (the tallest bar).
- Describe the **shape**: symmetrical, or with a tail to one side.
- Read frequencies, and add them for "how many were less than…".
- Compare two polygons: which is further right (higher values), which is more
  peaked (more consistent).

Watch out for a **broken scale** (a zigzag near the origin) or an axis that
doesn't start at zero: both exaggerate differences.

## Flashcards

### 1.3 | Why are there no gaps between the bars of a histogram?
The classes are continuous: one ends exactly where the next begins.

### 1.3 | What is plotted for a frequency polygon?
Frequency against **class midpoint**, joined by straight lines.

### 1.3 | What is the difference between a histogram and a bar chart?
A histogram shows continuous grouped data with touching bars; a bar chart
shows separate categories with gaps.

### 1.3 | With equal class widths, what does the height of a histogram bar show?
The **frequency** of that class.

### 1.8 | What does the tallest bar of a histogram tell you?
The **modal class**.

### 1.8 | Why can an axis that doesn't start at zero be misleading?
It exaggerates the differences between bars or points.

## Questions

### 1.3 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which diagram is most suitable for grouped continuous data?

- A. Pie chart
- B. Histogram
- C. Pictograph
- D. Bar chart

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Histograms are designed for continuous data grouped into classes.

### 1.3 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
For the class $30 \leq x < 40$ with frequency 12, what value is plotted on the
horizontal axis of a frequency polygon?

**Answer:** 35

**Working:** The class midpoint: $\frac{30 + 40}{2} = 35$.

### 1.8 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A histogram of test marks has its tallest bar over $50 \leq m < 60$. What does
this show?

- A. The median is 55.
- B. The mean is 55.
- C. The modal class is $50 \leq m < 60$.
- D. Everyone scored between 50 and 60.

**Answer:** C

**Working:** The tallest bar is the class with the highest frequency: the modal
class. It doesn't fix the mean or the median.

### 1.8 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 3
Two frequency polygons are drawn on the same axes, showing the marks of Class
X and Class Y in the same test. Class X's polygon peaks at 45 marks; Class Y's
peaks at 65 marks and is narrower.

(a) Which class performed better overall? Give a reason. **(2 marks)**

(b) Which class's marks were more consistent? Give a reason. **(2 marks)**

(c) Name one feature of the axes you would check before trusting the
comparison. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) Class Y — its polygon peaks at a higher mark, so more students
scored highly. (b) Class Y — its polygon is narrower, so the marks are less
spread out. (c) That both use the same scales, and that the frequency axis
starts at zero.
