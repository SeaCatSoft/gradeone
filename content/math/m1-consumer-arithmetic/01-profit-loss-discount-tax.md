## Lesson

Consumer arithmetic is the maths of buying and selling. It needs almost
nothing new — mostly percentages of a quantity — but it has its own
vocabulary, and knowing exactly what each word means is half the battle.

### Cost price, selling price, profit and loss

- **Cost price (CP):** what the seller paid for the item.
- **Selling price (SP):** what the seller sells it for.

If the selling price is higher, the seller makes a **profit**. If it is lower,
a **loss**.

$$\text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} \qquad \text{Loss} = \text{CP} - \text{SP}$$

A vendor buys a phone case for \$24 and sells it for \$30. Profit
$= 30 - 24 = \$6$.

### Percentage profit and loss

A profit of \$6 is impressive on a \$24 case and trivial on a \$2 000 laptop.
Percentages make them comparable. The rule that matters most:

> Percentage profit and loss are worked out on the **cost price**.

$$\text{Percentage profit} = \frac{\text{profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100\% \qquad \text{Percentage loss} = \frac{\text{loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100\%$$

For the phone case: $\dfrac{6}{24} \times 100\% = 25\%$ profit.

Dividing by the selling price instead ($\frac{6}{30} = 20\%$) is the most common
error in the whole topic. Unless a question says otherwise, the cost price is
the whole.

### Discount

A **discount** is an amount taken off the **marked price** — the price on the
label.

> A dress is marked \$180, with a 15% discount.

$$\text{Discount} = 15\% \times 180 = 0.15 \times 180 = \$27$$
$$\text{Price paid} = 180 - 27 = \$153$$

A faster way, in one step: a 15% discount means you pay **85%** of the marked
price. $0.85 \times 180 = \$153$.

### Sales tax

**Sales tax** (VAT in many Caribbean countries) is added **on top of** the
price.

> A blender costs \$220 before tax. Sales tax is 12.5%.

$$\text{Tax} = 0.125 \times 220 = \$27.50$$
$$\text{Price paid} = 220 + 27.50 = \$247.50$$

Or in one step: pay **112.5%** of the price. $1.125 \times 220 = \$247.50$.

### Discount and tax together

When a question has both, do them **in the order it describes** — usually the
discount first, then tax on the reduced price.

> Marked price \$400, 10% discount, then 15% sales tax.

- After discount: $0.90 \times 400 = \$360$
- After tax: $1.15 \times 360 = \$414$

A 10% discount and a 15% tax do **not** simply combine into a 5% increase.
That would give \$420. Each percentage acts on a different amount.

## Flashcards

### 2.1 | What is the formula for profit?
$$\text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP}$$

Selling price minus cost price.

### 2.2 | Percentage profit is calculated on which price?
The **cost price**.

$$\frac{\text{profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100\%$$

> Hint: it is the price the seller started with.

### 2.1 | Which is added to a price, and which is taken off: discount or sales tax?
A **discount** is taken off. **Sales tax** is added on.

### 2.1 | An item is marked \$60 with a 20% discount. What do you pay?
\$48. With 20% off you pay 80%: $0.8 \times 60 = 48$.

### 2.1 | A price is \$80 before 15% sales tax. What is the price after tax?
\$92. With tax you pay 115%: $1.15 \times 80 = 92$.

### 2.2 | Bought for \$50, sold for \$40. What is the percentage loss?
$$\frac{10}{50} \times 100\% = 20\%$$

## Questions

### 2.1 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 1
A shopkeeper buys a school bag for \$45 and sells it for \$63. What is the
profit, in dollars?

**Answer:** 18

**Working:** Profit $= \text{SP} - \text{CP} = 63 - 45 = 18$.

### 2.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A bicycle bought for \$400 is sold for \$340. What is the percentage loss?

- A. 15%
- B. 17.6%
- C. 60%
- D. 85%

**Answer:** A

**Working:** Loss $= 400 - 340 = 60$. Percentage loss
$= \frac{60}{400} \times 100\% = 15\%$.

B divides by the selling price, 340, instead of the cost price. D is the
selling price as a percentage of the cost price.

### 2.1 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A pair of shoes is marked \$250. A 30% discount is given. How much is paid, in
dollars?

**Answer:** 175

**Working:** With 30% off, the customer pays 70%: $0.7 \times 250 = 175$.

### 2.1 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A radio costs \$160 before sales tax. Sales tax is charged at 12.5%. What is the
total price, in dollars?

**Answer:** 180

**Working:** Tax $= 0.125 \times 160 = 20$. Total $= 160 + 20 = 180$.

### 2.2 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A trader buys 50 mangoes for \$40 and sells all of them at \$1.20 each. Find
her percentage profit.

**Answer:** 50

**Working:** Selling price of all 50: $50 \times 1.20 = \$60$.

Profit $= 60 - 40 = \$20$. Percentage profit $= \frac{20}{40} \times 100\% = 50\%$.

Compare totals with totals: the cost is for 50 mangoes, so the selling price
must be too.

### 2.1 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 3
A television is marked at \$2 400. During a sale, the store gives a 20%
discount. Sales tax of 15% is then charged on the discounted price.

(a) Calculate the amount of the discount. **(1 mark)**

(b) Calculate the price after the discount. **(1 mark)**

(c) Calculate the final price, including sales tax. **(2 marks)**

(d) Kevin says, "20% off and then 15% on is the same as 5% off." Show that he is
wrong. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) \$480 (b) \$1920 (c) \$2208 (d) 5% off would give \$2280, not
\$2208.

**Working:** (a) $0.20 \times 2400 = 480$.

(b) $2400 - 480 = 1920$.

(c) Tax $= 0.15 \times 1920 = 288$ (1 mark). Final price
$= 1920 + 288 = 2208$ (1 mark).

(d) 5% off \$2 400 is $0.95 \times 2400 = \$2280$, which is not \$2 208. The
percentages act on different amounts: the 15% is taken of \$1 920, not of
\$2 400.
