## Lesson

### Markup

A shop buys goods at cost price and adds an amount to set its selling price.
That amount is the **markup**.

$$\text{Selling price} = \text{cost price} + \text{markup}$$

Markup is usually given as a percentage **of the cost price**.

> A shop buys a kettle for \$80 and marks it up by 35%.

$$\text{Markup} = 0.35 \times 80 = \$28 \qquad \text{Selling price} = 80 + 28 = \$108$$

Or in one step: $1.35 \times 80 = \$108$.

### Expressing an amount as a percentage

Every "as a percentage" question is the same calculation:

$$\frac{\text{the amount}}{\text{the value it is a percentage of}} \times 100\%$$

The only skill is choosing the right bottom number:

| Percentage of… | Divide by |
|---|---|
| profit, loss or markup | the **cost price** |
| discount | the **marked price** (the original, undiscounted price) |
| sales or purchase tax | the price **before tax** |

A \$36 discount on a \$240 item is $\frac{36}{240} \times 100\% = 15\%$.

### Working backwards: finding the original price

This is where most marks are lost. Suppose

> After a 20% discount, a jacket costs \$96. What was the marked price?

The tempting answer is to take 20% of \$96 and add it back: $96 + 19.20 = \$115.20$.
**That's wrong.** The 20% was taken of the marked price, not of \$96.

Think instead: after a 20% discount, the customer pays **80%** of the marked
price. So \$96 is 80%.

$$80\% \to \$96 \qquad 1\% \to \frac{96}{80} = \$1.20 \qquad 100\% \to \$120$$

Check: 20% off \$120 is $120 - 24 = \$96$. ✓

The same method works for anything that has already had a percentage applied:

| Situation | The final price is | So divide by |
|---|---|---|
| 20% discount | 80% of the original | 0.80 |
| 15% tax added | 115% of the price before tax | 1.15 |
| 25% profit | 125% of the cost price | 1.25 |
| 10% loss | 90% of the cost price | 0.90 |

> A phone was sold for \$690 at a profit of 15%. What was the cost price?

Selling price $= 115\%$ of the cost price, so
$\text{CP} = 690 \div 1.15 = \$600$.

### A checklist for longer problems

1. Label every price you're given: **CP**, **SP** or **marked price**.
2. Decide which price each percentage is **of**.
3. If a percentage has already been applied, work backwards by dividing.
4. Check by working forwards from your answer.

## Flashcards

### 2.3 | Markup percentage is a percentage of which price?
The **cost price**.

### 2.3 | A discount percentage is a percentage of which price?
The **marked price**: the original price before the discount.

### 2.4 | After a 25% discount an item costs \$60. What was the marked price?
\$80. The \$60 is 75% of the marked price: $60 \div 0.75 = 80$.

> Hint: don't add 25% of \$60 back on.

### 2.4 | Sold for \$132 at a 10% profit. What was the cost price?
\$120. The selling price is 110% of the cost price: $132 \div 1.10 = 120$.

### 2.4 | A price including 15% tax is \$230. What was the price before tax?
\$200. $230 \div 1.15 = 200$.

## Questions

### 2.3 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A store buys a lamp for \$64 and sells it for \$80. Express the markup as a
percentage of the cost price.

**Answer:** 25

**Working:** Markup $= 80 - 64 = 16$.
$\frac{16}{64} \times 100\% = 25\%$.

### 2.3 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A \$450 sofa is sold for \$378 in a sale. What percentage discount was given?

- A. 16%
- B. 19%
- C. 72%
- D. 84%

**Answer:** A

**Working:** Discount $= 450 - 378 = 72$. As a percentage of the marked price:
$\frac{72}{450} \times 100\% = 16\%$.

B divides by the sale price, \$378. D is the sale price as a percentage of the
marked price.

### 2.4 | numeric | 1 mark | difficulty 3
After a discount of 30%, a pair of trainers costs \$168. What was the marked
price, in dollars?

**Answer:** 240

**Working:** With 30% off, \$168 is 70% of the marked price.
$168 \div 0.7 = 240$.

Check: 30% of 240 is 72, and $240 - 72 = 168$. ✓

### 2.4 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A car was sold for \$36 000, making a loss of 20%. What was the cost price?

- A. \$28 800
- B. \$43 200
- C. \$45 000
- D. \$56 000

**Answer:** C

**Working:** A 20% loss means the selling price is 80% of the cost price.
$36\,000 \div 0.8 = 45\,000$.

B adds 20% of \$36 000 back on, which uses the wrong whole.

### 2.4 | structured | 5 marks | difficulty 4
A wholesaler sells a laptop to a shop for \$1 800. The shop marks it up by 40%.
Later, in a sale, the shop reduces the marked price by 15%.

(a) Calculate the marked price. **(1 mark)**

(b) Calculate the sale price. **(2 marks)**

(c) Calculate the shop's profit in the sale, as a percentage of its cost
price. **(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) \$2520 (b) \$2142 (c) 19%

**Working:** (a) $1.40 \times 1800 = 2520$.

(b) With 15% off, the customer pays 85%: $0.85 \times 2520 = 2142$. (1 mark for
the method, 1 for the answer.)

(c) Profit $= 2142 - 1800 = 342$. As a percentage of the cost price:
$\frac{342}{1800} \times 100\% = 19\%$. (1 mark for 342, 1 for 19%.)

A 40% markup followed by a 15% reduction leaves 19% profit, not 25%.
