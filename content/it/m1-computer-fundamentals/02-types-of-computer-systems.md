## Lesson

Computers are classified by **size and power**, and the syllabus asks you to
compare them on three measures in particular: **processing speed**, **storage
capacity** and **portability**.

Those three tend to pull against one another. The fastest machines with the
most storage are the least portable, and the most portable are the slowest.
Almost every question on this objective turns on that trade-off.

### Supercomputers

The fastest computers in existence, built for problems that need enormous
amounts of calculation: weather forecasting, climate modelling, nuclear
simulation, genetic research.

- **Speed**: the highest available — measured in floating-point operations per
  second, on a scale no other class approaches.
- **Storage**: enormous.
- **Portability**: none. A supercomputer fills a room and needs dedicated
  cooling and power.
- **Cost**: millions, so they are owned by governments, large research
  institutes and universities.
- **Users**: a single problem at a time, worked on by a team.

### Mainframes

Large, powerful computers built to serve **many users at once** and to process
huge volumes of transactions reliably: banks, airlines, insurance companies,
government tax systems.

- **Speed**: very fast — but their strength is *throughput*, the volume of work
  handled, rather than raw calculating speed.
- **Storage**: very large.
- **Portability**: none. A mainframe occupies a room.
- **Users**: hundreds or thousands at the same time, through terminals.

The distinction between a supercomputer and a mainframe is examined. A
**supercomputer** does one enormous calculation very fast. A **mainframe**
handles a vast number of ordinary transactions at once, without stopping.
Weather forecast against bank accounts.

### Servers

A computer that provides services to other computers on a network — files, web
pages, email, databases. More powerful than a desktop, less so than a
mainframe, and designed to run continuously.

- **Speed**: high.
- **Storage**: large, often with discs arranged so that one failing loses
  nothing.
- **Portability**: none in practice; they are racked in a room.

### Desktop computers (microcomputers)

The ordinary personal computer: a case, monitor, keyboard and mouse, used by
one person at a time.

- **Speed**: adequate for everyday work — office software, browsing, media.
- **Storage**: substantial, and easily extended.
- **Portability**: low. It can be moved, but not while in use.
- **Advantages over portables**: cheaper for the same power, easier to upgrade
  and repair, a full-sized screen and keyboard.

### Laptops (notebooks)

A complete computer in one hinged case, with a built-in screen, keyboard and
battery.

- **Speed**: close to a desktop, usually a little lower for the same money.
- **Storage**: similar to a desktop, sometimes less.
- **Portability**: good — it can be carried and used anywhere.
- **Trade-offs**: costs more than a desktop of the same power, is harder to
  upgrade, and is easier to steal or damage.

### Tablets

A flat device that is mostly screen, operated by touch.

- **Speed**: lower than a laptop.
- **Storage**: less, and often not expandable.
- **Portability**: very good — light, with long battery life and instant
  start.
- **Best for**: reading, browsing, watching, light note-taking. Poor for long
  typing or heavy work.

### Smartphones

A mobile telephone that is also a computer, with a touchscreen, camera and
network connection.

- **Speed**: lower than a tablet, though modern ones are surprisingly capable.
- **Storage**: the least of the general-purpose classes.
- **Portability**: the best — it is carried everywhere.
- **Significance**: for many people, and in many countries, a phone is the
  *only* computer they own and their only route onto the internet.

### Embedded systems

A computer built into another device to control it: a washing machine, a
microwave, a car's engine management, a traffic light, a fitness band.

- **Speed**: low — only what its single task requires.
- **Storage**: very small.
- **Portability**: it goes wherever the device goes.
- **Characteristic**: it does one job, its program is fixed, and the user
  usually does not think of it as a computer at all. They are by far the most
  numerous computers in the world.

### The comparison

| Type | Speed | Storage | Portability | Typical users |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supercomputer | Highest | Enormous | None | Research institutes |
| Mainframe | Very high throughput | Very large | None | Banks, airlines |
| Server | High | Large | None | Organisations |
| Desktop | Moderate | Large | Low | One person |
| Laptop | Moderate | Large | Good | One person, mobile |
| Tablet | Lower | Smaller | Very good | Consumer, reading |
| Smartphone | Lower still | Smallest | Best | Everyone |
| Embedded | Lowest | Tiny | With its device | Nobody directly |

Read it down the columns: speed and storage fall as portability rises. That
pattern is the answer to most questions on this objective.

### Choosing for a purpose

Questions usually describe a need and ask which type suits it. Match the
requirement to the measure that matters:

| Need | Choice | Because |
|---|---|---|
| National weather forecasting | Supercomputer | Enormous calculation |
| A bank's account transactions | Mainframe | Huge volume, many users, reliability |
| Hosting a school's website | Server | Runs continuously, serves many requests |
| A school computer lab | Desktops | Cheapest per machine, easy to repair |
| A sales representative on the road | Laptop | Full computer, portable |
| Reading and note-taking in lectures | Tablet | Light, long battery |
| Controlling a washing machine | Embedded | One fixed task, tiny cost |

## Flashcards

### 1.2 | On which three measures does the syllabus compare computer types?
**Processing speed**, **storage capacity** and **portability**.

### 1.2 | What is the general relationship between portability and power?
They pull against each other: as portability rises, processing speed and
storage capacity fall.

### 1.2 | What is a supercomputer used for?
Problems needing enormous amounts of calculation — weather forecasting, climate
modelling, nuclear simulation, genetic research.

### 1.2 | Distinguish between a supercomputer and a mainframe.
A **supercomputer** performs one enormous calculation extremely fast. A
**mainframe** handles a vast number of ordinary transactions for many users at
once.

> Hint: weather forecast against bank accounts.

### 1.2 | What is a server?
A computer that provides services — files, web pages, email, databases — to
other computers on a network, and is designed to run continuously.

### 1.2 | Give two advantages of a desktop over a laptop.
Any two: cheaper for the same power; easier to upgrade and repair; a
full-sized screen and keyboard; less easily stolen.

### 1.2 | Give two disadvantages of a laptop compared with a desktop.
Any two: costs more for the same power; harder to upgrade; more easily damaged
or stolen; smaller screen and keyboard.

### 1.2 | How does a tablet compare with a laptop?
Lower processing speed and less storage, but lighter, with longer battery life
and instant start. Poor for long typing.

### 1.2 | Why are smartphones significant beyond their specifications?
For many people they are the only computer they own and their only route onto
the internet.

### 1.2 | What is an embedded system? Give three examples.
A computer built into another device to control it — a washing machine, a
microwave, a car's engine management, a traffic light.

### 1.2 | What is characteristic of an embedded system?
It does one fixed job, has very little storage and processing power, and the
user does not think of it as a computer. They are the most numerous computers
in the world.

### 1.2 | Which computer type suits a bank processing millions of transactions for many users?
A **mainframe** — built for high throughput, many simultaneous users and
continuous reliable operation.

## Questions

### 1.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which type of computer has the greatest processing speed?

- A. Mainframe
- B. Supercomputer
- C. Server
- D. Desktop

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Supercomputers are the fastest machines built. A mainframe's
strength is the volume of transactions it handles, not raw calculating speed.

### 1.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A national airline needs a computer to handle bookings from thousands of agents
at the same time, without interruption. The most suitable type is

- A. a supercomputer
- B. a mainframe
- C. a desktop
- D. a tablet

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Many users, huge transaction volume and continuous reliability
describe a mainframe. A supercomputer is built for calculation, not throughput.

### 1.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which statement about portability and processing power is generally true?

- A. The most portable devices have the greatest processing power
- B. As portability increases, processing speed and storage tend to decrease
- C. Portability has no effect on storage capacity
- D. Tablets are more powerful than desktops

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Smaller devices have less room for components, less power and less
cooling, so speed and storage fall as portability rises.

### 1.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A washing machine contains a computer that controls its cycles. This is an
example of

- A. a server
- B. a mainframe
- C. an embedded system
- D. a tablet

**Answer:** C

**Working:** An embedded system is built into another device to control it,
performs one fixed task, and is not thought of as a computer by its user.

### 1.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A school must equip a computer laboratory with 30 machines on a limited budget.
Machines will stay in the room and must be easy to repair. The best choice is

- A. tablets
- B. laptops
- C. desktops
- D. servers

**Answer:** C

**Working:** Portability is not needed, so its cost can be avoided. Desktops
give the most power per dollar and their parts can be replaced individually.

### 1.2 | structured | 10 marks | difficulty 3
(a) Compare a **desktop computer** and a **tablet** in terms of processing
speed, storage capacity and portability. **(3 marks)**

(b) State the most suitable type of computer system for each of the following,
with a reason:

(i) forecasting the weather for the whole Caribbean **(2 marks)**

(ii) processing a bank's daily transactions for thousands of customers
**(2 marks)**

(iii) controlling the temperature in a microwave oven **(2 marks)**

(c) Give **one** reason why a sales representative who travels daily would
choose a laptop rather than a desktop. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) 1 mark each: the desktop has greater processing speed; the
desktop has greater storage capacity; the tablet is far more portable.
(b)(i) 1 mark — a supercomputer; 1 mark — weather forecasting requires enormous
amounts of calculation, which only a supercomputer can perform quickly enough.
(ii) 1 mark — a mainframe; 1 mark — it handles a very large volume of
transactions for many users at once, reliably and continuously.
(iii) 1 mark — an embedded system; 1 mark — it performs one fixed control task
within another device, needing very little processing power or storage.
(c) A laptop is portable and can be used anywhere, including while travelling
or at a customer's premises — 1 mark.

**Working:** (a) All three measures are needed. Note that the comparison must
say which is greater, not merely that they differ.

(b)(i) and (ii) are the pair most often confused. The test is whether the work
is one huge calculation (supercomputer) or a great many small ones for many
users (mainframe).
