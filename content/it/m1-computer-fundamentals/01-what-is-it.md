## Lesson

**Information Technology (IT)** is the use of computers and telecommunications
equipment to store, retrieve, process and transmit information.

Four verbs, and they are worth learning as a set, because a definition that
names them all scores and one that says "using computers" does not.

| Verb | Means |
|---|---|
| **Store** | Keep data for later — on a disc, in a database, in the cloud |
| **Retrieve** | Get it back when needed — a query, a search |
| **Process** | Turn it into something more useful — calculate, sort, summarise |
| **Transmit** | Send it somewhere else — email, a network, the web |

### Technology, and telecommunications

Note that the definition names **telecommunications** as well as computers. A
computer that cannot communicate is a far smaller thing than one that can, and
almost all modern IT involves moving information between places: a bank card
read in a shop, a message delivered to a phone, a lesson loaded from a server.

This is why the field is sometimes called **ICT** — Information and
Communications Technology. The two terms are used interchangeably in most
contexts; ICT simply makes the communication part explicit.

### What IT is made of

IT is not only machines. It has four components, and leaving any of them out
misdescribes the field:

**Hardware** — the physical equipment: computers, phones, printers, cables,
servers.

**Software** — the programs telling the hardware what to do.

**Data** — the raw facts the whole system exists to work with.

**People** — those who design, build, operate and use the systems. Sometimes
listed with **procedures**, the rules for how the system is used.

A bank's IT system is not its computers. It is the computers, the programs, the
account records, the staff who run it and the rules they follow. Remove any one
and nothing works.

### Where IT is used

The objective asks you to explain the *concept*, and examples are what make an
explanation convincing:

| Field | Use of IT |
|---|---|
| Education | Online lessons, student records, research |
| Banking | ATMs, online banking, fraud detection |
| Health | Patient records, scanning equipment, telemedicine |
| Business | Stock control, payroll, e-commerce |
| Government | Tax systems, national ID, public information |
| Entertainment | Streaming, games, digital music |
| Transport | Ticketing, navigation, traffic control |
| Agriculture | Weather data, irrigation control, market prices |

### Why IT matters

**Speed.** A calculation that would take a person a week takes a moment.

**Accuracy.** A computer does not mis-add a column of figures. (It does exactly
what it was told, which is a different matter — that is where errors come
from.)

**Storage.** A building's worth of paper records fits on one disc, and can be
searched in seconds.

**Communication.** Information reaches anywhere in the world almost
immediately.

**Automation.** Repetitive work is done without human effort, freeing people
for work that needs judgement.

**Access.** Information and services reach people who were previously too far
from them — a farmer checking market prices, a student taking a course from
another country.

### And the other side

An honest account of IT includes its costs, and examination questions ask for
them:

- **Job displacement** where automation replaces routine work.
- **Privacy**: systems that hold data about people can be misused or breached.
- **Security**: everything connected can be attacked.
- **The digital divide** between those with access to technology and those
  without — which can widen existing inequality rather than reduce it.
- **Dependence**: when the system fails, the work stops, and few organisations
  can still operate on paper.
- **Health and environmental effects**, from screen time to electronic waste.

These are the subject of Section 3, and it is worth knowing now that the
syllabus treats them as part of understanding IT, not as an afterthought.

### A computer, defined

Since the definition of IT rests on it: a **computer** is an electronic device
that accepts data as input, processes it according to stored instructions,
produces information as output, and can store both.

That sentence contains the **IPOS cycle** — Input, Processing, Output, Storage
— which is the organising idea of this whole section and gets a lesson of its
own.

The phrase *according to stored instructions* is what separates a computer from
a calculator: it holds a program, and changing the program changes what the
machine does without changing the machine.

## Flashcards

### 1.1 | Define Information Technology.
The use of computers and telecommunications equipment to **store**, **retrieve**,
**process** and **transmit** information.

### 1.1 | Name the four things IT does with information.
Stores it, retrieves it, processes it, and transmits it.

### 1.1 | What does ICT stand for, and how does it differ from IT?
**Information and Communications Technology**. It makes the communications
element explicit; the two terms are largely interchangeable.

### 1.1 | Name the four components of an IT system.
**Hardware**, **software**, **data** and **people** (with procedures often
listed alongside people).

### 1.1 | Why is it wrong to say a bank's IT system is its computers?
The system is the hardware, the software, the data, the staff who operate it
and the procedures they follow. Remove any one and nothing works.

### 1.1 | Give four benefits of IT.
Any four: speed; accuracy; large storage in little space; fast communication;
automation of repetitive work; wider access to information and services.

### 1.1 | Give four drawbacks of IT.
Any four: job displacement through automation; threats to privacy; security
risks; the digital divide; dependence on systems that can fail; health and
environmental effects.

### 1.1 | What is the digital divide?
The gap between those with access to technology and those without, which can
widen existing inequalities.

### 1.1 | Define a computer.
An electronic device that accepts data as input, processes it according to
stored instructions, produces information as output, and can store both.

### 1.1 | What separates a computer from a calculator?
A computer works from **stored instructions** — a program — so changing the
program changes what it does without changing the machine.

### 1.1 | What does IPOS stand for?
**Input, Processing, Output, Storage** — the cycle describing how a computer
system works.

## Questions

### 1.1 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Information Technology is best defined as the use of computers and
telecommunications equipment to

- A. build machines and repair them
- B. store, retrieve, process and transmit information
- C. write programs in a high-level language
- D. connect computers into a network

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The four verbs are the definition. Options C and D are activities
within IT, not the whole of it.

### 1.1 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which of the following is **not** one of the four components of an IT system?

- A. Hardware
- B. Software
- C. Data
- D. Electricity

**Answer:** D

**Working:** The components are hardware, software, data and people (with
procedures). Electricity powers the equipment but is not a component of the
system in this sense.

### 1.1 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A supermarket's system records each sale, updates stock levels and sends an
order to the supplier when stock runs low. Which IT functions are being
performed?

- A. Storing only
- B. Storing and processing only
- C. Storing, processing and transmitting
- D. Transmitting only

**Answer:** C

**Working:** The sale is stored, the stock level is processed to decide whether
to reorder, and the order is transmitted to the supplier.

### 1.1 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
The gap between people who have access to modern technology and those who do
not is known as

- A. the digital divide
- B. automation
- C. the IPOS cycle
- D. data processing

**Answer:** A

**Working:** The digital divide is one of the social costs of IT, because it
can widen inequality rather than reduce it.

### 1.1 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
What distinguishes a computer from a simple calculator?

- A. It is electronic
- B. It works from stored instructions that can be changed
- C. It performs arithmetic
- D. It has a keyboard

**Answer:** B

**Working:** A calculator is also electronic, also does arithmetic and may also
have a keyboard. Holding a program — so that changing the program changes what
the machine does — is what makes a computer general-purpose.

### 1.1 | structured | 9 marks | difficulty 3
(a) Define the term **Information Technology**. **(2 marks)**

(b) Name the **four** components of an IT system. **(4 marks)**

(c) State **one** benefit and **one** drawback of the increasing use of IT in a
developing country, explaining each briefly. **(3 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) 1 mark — the use of computers and telecommunications
equipment; 1 mark — to store, retrieve, process and transmit information (at
least three of the four verbs).
(b) 1 mark each: hardware; software; data; people (accept procedures as an
alternative to people only if people are also given, in which case award the
mark for either).
(c) 1 mark for a benefit and 1 for a drawback, plus 1 for a developed
explanation. Benefits: access to information and services for people far from
them; faster communication; automation freeing people for other work; better
record-keeping. Drawbacks: the digital divide where many lack access;
displacement of routine jobs; dependence on systems that can fail; security and
privacy risks; cost of equipment and training.

**Working:** (a) "Using computers" alone does not earn the second mark. The
verbs are what make the definition.

(c) Tie the answer to the setting named in the question. "The digital divide
widens because only those who can afford devices benefit" is a stronger answer
than "IT can be expensive".
