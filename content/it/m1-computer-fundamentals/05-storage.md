## Lesson

Data has to live somewhere when the computer is switched off. There are two
places: **local storage**, on a device you hold, and **cloud storage**, on
somebody else's servers, reached over the internet.

The objective says *evaluate*, which means weighing both sides rather than
listing features. Examination answers that give only advantages score half.

### Local storage

Storage on a device in your possession:

| Medium | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Hard disc drive (HDD) | The computer's main storage; cheap per gigabyte |
| Solid state drive (SSD) | Faster, no moving parts, costs more |
| USB flash drive | Carrying files between machines |
| Memory card | Cameras, phones |
| Optical disc (CD, DVD) | Distribution; now largely replaced |
| Magnetic tape | Large organisational backups; very cheap, very slow |
| External hard drive | Backups and extra capacity |

**Advantages**

- **No internet needed.** The files are there whether or not you are connected.
- **Fast.** Reading from an internal drive is far quicker than downloading, and
  the difference grows with file size.
- **No ongoing cost.** You buy the drive once.
- **You control it.** The data is not in anyone else's hands, which matters for
  confidential material.
- **No limit but the hardware.** Buy a bigger drive.

**Disadvantages**

- **It can be lost.** A failed drive, a stolen laptop, a fire or a flood takes
  the data with it. This is the serious one.
- **Reachable only from that device**, unless you carry it about.
- **Sharing is awkward** — files must be copied or emailed.
- **You do the backups**, and most people do not.
- **Capacity must be planned** and bought in advance.

### Cloud storage

Storage on remote servers, run by a provider, reached over the internet —
Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, iCloud.

**Advantages**

- **Access from anywhere**, on any device with a connection.
- **Automatic backup** by the provider, usually with copies in more than one
  place.
- **Easy sharing** — send a link rather than a file, and several people can
  work on one document at once.
- **Scalable.** Need more room, pay for more room; no hardware to buy.
- **Survives your device.** If the laptop is stolen, the files are not.
- **Synchronisation** keeps the same files on your phone, tablet and computer.
- **Version history** lets you recover an earlier copy of a file.

**Disadvantages**

- **Requires an internet connection.** No connection, no files — and this is
  the decisive disadvantage in places where connectivity is unreliable or
  expensive.
- **Speed depends on the connection**, and uploading a large file may take
  hours.
- **Ongoing cost.** Free tiers are small; beyond them it is a subscription
  that never ends.
- **Security and privacy.** The data sits on someone else's equipment, and a
  breach of the provider is a breach of your data.
- **You depend on the provider.** If it raises prices, changes terms or closes,
  you must act.
- **Data uses bandwidth**, which is a real cost on a metered mobile connection.
- **Legal questions** about where the data is physically held and whose laws
  apply.

### Side by side

| | Local storage | Cloud storage |
|---|---|---|
| Internet needed | No | Yes |
| Access from other devices | No | Yes |
| Speed | Fast | Depends on connection |
| Cost | One-off purchase | Ongoing subscription |
| Backup | Your responsibility | Handled by the provider |
| Sharing | Awkward | Easy — send a link |
| Control of the data | Yours | With the provider |
| Risk if device is lost | Data lost too | Data unaffected |
| Capacity | Limited by hardware | Expands on demand |

### Choosing between them

The choice depends on the situation, and examination questions give you one:

| Situation | Better choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Unreliable or costly internet | Local | Cloud files are unreachable when offline |
| A team working on shared documents | Cloud | Several people, one current copy |
| Highly confidential records | Local, with strong backups | The data stays in your hands |
| Editing large video files | Local | Speed; uploading would take hours |
| A student wanting work on school and home computers | Cloud | Access from anywhere |
| Protecting against fire or theft | Cloud, or an off-site backup | A local backup burns with the original |

### The answer is usually both

In practice, organisations use **both**, and saying so is often the best answer
to an evaluation question.

The standard advice for backups is the **3-2-1 rule**: keep **3** copies of
important data, on **2** different kinds of media, with **1** of them stored
somewhere else. Cloud storage is a convenient way to be that one off-site copy.

The reasoning behind it is worth carrying: a backup on the same disc as the
original is no backup at all, and a backup in the same building as the original
is no protection against fire, flood or theft.

### A note on what "the cloud" actually is

"The cloud" sounds weightless and is not. Cloud storage is **other people's
computers** — racks of servers in data centres, with power, cooling and staff.
The word describes who looks after them, not what they are.

It matters because it explains both sides of the evaluation. The provider's
scale is why backups and redundancy come free; the fact that it is someone
else's equipment is why control and privacy are the concerns they are.

## Flashcards

### 1.5 | What is local storage?
Storage on a device in your own possession — an internal hard disc, an SSD, a
USB flash drive, a memory card, an external drive.

### 1.5 | What is cloud storage?
Storage on remote servers run by a provider and reached over the internet, such
as Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox.

### 1.5 | Give four advantages of cloud storage.
Any four: access from anywhere and any device; automatic backup by the
provider; easy sharing and collaboration; capacity expands on demand; data
survives the loss of your device; synchronisation across devices; version
history.

### 1.5 | Give four disadvantages of cloud storage.
Any four: it needs an internet connection; speed depends on that connection;
ongoing subscription cost; the data is held on someone else's equipment,
raising security and privacy concerns; dependence on the provider; bandwidth
costs.

### 1.5 | Give three advantages of local storage.
Any three: no internet connection needed; fast access; no ongoing cost; you
keep control of the data; capacity limited only by the hardware you buy.

### 1.5 | Give three disadvantages of local storage.
Any three: the data is lost if the device fails, is stolen or is damaged; it
can only be reached from that device; sharing is awkward; you must do your own
backups; capacity must be bought in advance.

### 1.5 | Which storage would suit an area with unreliable internet, and why?
**Local** — cloud files cannot be reached without a connection, so work would
stop whenever the connection did.

### 1.5 | Which storage suits a team working on shared documents, and why?
**Cloud** — several people can reach one current copy, and sharing needs only a
link rather than emailed copies that drift out of step.

### 1.5 | What is the 3-2-1 backup rule?
Keep **3** copies of important data, on **2** different kinds of media, with
**1** stored somewhere else.

### 1.5 | Why is a backup in the same building as the original inadequate?
It gives no protection against fire, flood or theft, which would destroy both
copies together.

### 1.5 | What is "the cloud", physically?
Racks of servers in data centres — other people's computers. The word describes
who maintains them, not what they are.

> Hint: it explains both the free backups and the privacy concern.

## Questions

### 1.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which is the main disadvantage of cloud storage?

- A. It cannot store large files
- B. It requires an internet connection
- C. It cannot be shared
- D. It has no backup

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Without a connection the files are unreachable. Sharing and
backup are among cloud storage's strengths.

### 1.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A photographer must edit several very large video files each day, in a studio
with slow internet. The better choice is

- A. cloud storage, for the automatic backup
- B. local storage, because access is fast and needs no connection
- C. cloud storage, because it is cheaper
- D. optical discs, because they are portable

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Large files over a slow connection would take hours to transfer.
Local storage gives the speed the work needs — though the studio should still
keep an off-site backup.

### 1.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A student wants to work on the same project at school and at home without
carrying anything. The better choice is

- A. local storage on the school computer
- B. cloud storage
- C. an internal hard disc
- D. magnetic tape

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Access from any device with a connection is exactly what cloud
storage provides.

### 1.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A laptop holding a company's only copy of its accounts is stolen. Which
statement is correct?

- A. Cloud storage would have prevented the theft
- B. The data would have survived had it also been in cloud storage
- C. Local storage is always safer than cloud storage
- D. The data can be recovered from RAM

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Cloud storage cannot stop a laptop being taken, but the files
would not have been lost with it. This is the strongest argument for an
off-site copy.

### 1.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Under the 3-2-1 backup rule, how many copies should be kept off-site?

- A. Three
- B. Two
- C. One
- D. None

**Answer:** C

**Working:** Three copies in total, on two kinds of media, with one of them
elsewhere — so that a fire or theft at one location cannot destroy everything.

### 1.5 | structured | 10 marks | difficulty 3
A small law firm in a rural area keeps client records on the office computer's
hard disc. Its internet connection is slow and sometimes unavailable. The
partners are considering moving the records to cloud storage.

(a) State **two** advantages the firm would gain from cloud storage.
**(2 marks)**

(b) State **two** disadvantages, referring to the firm's situation.
**(4 marks)**

(c) Recommend what the firm should do, and justify your recommendation.
**(3 marks)**

(d) State **one** further precaution the firm should take whichever storage it
uses. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) 1 mark each, any two: records could be reached from anywhere;
backup is handled by the provider; records survive the loss, theft or failure
of the office computer; capacity expands as needed; files can be shared easily
between partners.
(b) 2 marks each for two developed disadvantages tied to the situation: the
connection is slow and sometimes unavailable, so records could not be reached
at all when the connection fails, stopping work; client records are
confidential and would be held on another company's servers, raising security,
privacy and legal concerns; cloud storage is an ongoing cost rather than a
one-off purchase.
(c) 3 marks: a recommendation (1) with justification (2). A strong answer keeps
the records locally for day-to-day work because of the unreliable connection
and their confidentiality, while using cloud storage — or another off-site copy
— as a backup, so the records survive fire or theft. Accept any defensible
recommendation that weighs both sides.
(d) Any one, 1 mark: keep regular backups; keep at least one copy off-site;
encrypt the records; follow the 3-2-1 rule.

**Working:** (b) Marks come from tying the disadvantage to the firm. "Cloud
storage needs internet" is worth one mark; "the connection is sometimes
unavailable, so the firm could not reach client records at all during a
consultation" earns both.

(c) The best answers do not choose one and dismiss the other. Using local
storage for working and cloud for backup addresses the connection problem and
the fire-and-theft problem at once.
