## Lesson

The objective is not to list devices but to **select** them: given an
application, say which device suits it and why. Questions describe a situation —
a supermarket, a hospital, a bank, a factory — and expect a device and a
justification.

### Input devices

**Keyboard** — typing text and numbers. Accurate for text, slow for bulk data,
and prone to typing errors.

**Mouse, trackpad, trackball** — pointing and selecting in a graphical
interface.

**Touchscreen** — pointing directly, with no separate device. Suits public
kiosks, phones and tablets: nothing to steal, nothing to break, and no
instructions needed.

**Scanner** — converts a printed page or photograph into an image. With **OCR**
software, printed text becomes editable text.

**Barcode reader** — reads the striped code on a product. Fast, and far more
accurate than typing a product number, which is why every supermarket uses one.

**QR code reader** — reads a two-dimensional code holding more data than a
barcode, commonly with a phone camera.

**Magnetic stripe reader** — reads the stripe on a bank or ID card.

**Chip and PIN reader** — reads a card's chip; more secure than the stripe
because the chip cannot easily be copied.

**MICR — magnetic ink character recognition** — reads the numbers printed along
the bottom of a cheque in magnetic ink. Used by banks because it is fast,
extremely accurate, and the characters can still be read when the cheque is
creased or written over.

**OMR — optical mark recognition** — detects pencil marks in boxes. Used for
multiple-choice examination papers, lottery tickets and survey forms, where
thousands of sheets must be read quickly.

**OCR — optical character recognition** — converts printed or written
characters in an image into text.

**Biometric readers** — fingerprint, face, iris or voice. Used where identity
must be proved: a fingerprint cannot be forgotten, lent or guessed the way a
password can.

**Microphone** — sound, and speech recognition.

**Digital camera / webcam** — still images and video.

**Sensors** — measure a physical quantity and send it as data: temperature,
light, pressure, humidity, motion. They are what make automatic control
possible, since they report continuously without anyone present.

**Graphics tablet** — a pen on a pressure-sensitive surface, for drawing and
design.

**Joystick / game controller** — games, and controlling machinery.

**Light pen, touchpad, remote control, RFID reader** — each for its own niche.

### Output devices

**Monitor (VDU)** — displays on screen. **Soft copy**: temporary, changes when
the display changes.

**Printer** — produces **hard copy** on paper:

| Printer | How it works | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| **Laser** | Toner fused by heat | High volume, fast, sharp text — an office |
| **Inkjet** | Sprays ink droplets | Low volume, good colour photographs — home |
| **Dot matrix** | Pins strike an inked ribbon | **Multi-part forms**, noisy, rugged — invoices, payslips |
| **Thermal** | Heats special paper | Receipts, small and quiet |
| **3D printer** | Builds an object in layers | Prototypes, models, parts |

The dot matrix survives for one reason worth knowing: it strikes the paper, so
it prints through carbon copies in one pass. No other printer can do that.

**Plotter** — draws with pens or a moving head on very large paper.
Architectural and engineering drawings.

**Speakers and headphones** — sound.

**Projector** — a large image for an audience.

**Actuators** — motors, valves and switches driven by a computer in a control
system. They are the output half of automatic control, as sensors are the input
half.

**Braille printer / display** — output for a blind user.

### Devices that are both

A **touchscreen** takes input and shows output. A **multifunction printer**
prints, scans and copies. A **headset** has both a microphone and speakers. A
**modem** sends and receives.

### Hard copy and soft copy

**Hard copy** is a permanent, physical form — printed paper. It can be read
without a computer and filed.

**Soft copy** is displayed electronically — on a screen. It can be edited and
shared easily, but is gone when the display changes and needs a device to read
it.

### Selecting a device: how to answer

Give the **device** and a **reason tied to the situation**. The reason is what
carries the marks.

| Application | Input | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Supermarket checkout | Barcode reader | Fast and accurate; avoids typing product codes |
| Marking multiple-choice examinations | OMR | Reads thousands of sheets quickly and accurately |
| Processing bank cheques | MICR | Fast, very accurate, readable even when the cheque is creased |
| Secure entry to a server room | Biometric reader | Identity cannot be lent, forgotten or guessed |
| Capturing a signed paper form | Scanner | Produces an image of the original document |
| Greenhouse temperature control | Temperature sensor | Measures continuously with nobody present |
| A public information kiosk | Touchscreen | Simple, needs no instructions, nothing to steal |
| Designing a building | Graphics tablet | Precise freehand drawing |

| Application | Output | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Printing 500 letters in an office | Laser printer | Fast, cheap per page, sharp text |
| Printing photographs at home | Inkjet printer | Good colour reproduction at low volume |
| Printing multi-part invoices | Dot matrix | Strikes the paper, so carbon copies print at once |
| Printing an architectural plan | Plotter | Handles very large paper accurately |
| Presenting to a class | Projector | A large image for many viewers |
| A supermarket receipt | Thermal printer | Small, quiet, fast, few moving parts |
| Opening a greenhouse vent | Actuator | Converts the computer's decision into movement |
| Output for a blind user | Braille printer or speech output | Readable without sight |

### Choosing for people, not just tasks

Some choices are about who is using the system.

A **blind user** needs speech output or a Braille display. A user who cannot
use a mouse may need **voice input** or a keyboard alternative. A user with
limited movement may need a **trackball** or a **switch**. A kiosk in a public
place needs a **touchscreen** because it must be usable without training.

Examination questions do ask this, and the answers are the same kind: name the
device, and say what about the person's situation makes it right.

## Flashcards

### 1.6 | What does an input device do?
Sends data into the computer for processing.

### 1.6 | Why does a supermarket use a barcode reader rather than a keyboard?
It is much faster and far more accurate — it removes the typing errors that
entering product codes by hand would produce.

### 1.6 | What is OMR and what is it used for?
**Optical mark recognition** — it detects pencil marks in boxes. Used for
multiple-choice examination papers, lottery tickets and surveys.

### 1.6 | What is MICR, and why do banks use it for cheques?
**Magnetic ink character recognition**. It is fast and extremely accurate, and
the magnetic characters can still be read when the cheque is creased or written
over.

### 1.6 | What is OCR?
**Optical character recognition** — converting printed or written characters in
an image into editable text.

### 1.6 | Give one advantage of a biometric reader over a password.
A fingerprint or iris cannot be forgotten, lent to someone else or guessed.

### 1.6 | What is a sensor, and why is it needed in a control system?
A device measuring a physical quantity — temperature, light, pressure — and
sending it as data. It reports continuously with nobody present.

### 1.6 | What is an actuator?
An output device — a motor, valve or switch — that turns a computer's decision
into physical movement in a control system.

### 1.6 | Distinguish between hard copy and soft copy.
**Hard copy** is permanent physical output, such as printed paper. **Soft
copy** is displayed electronically and needs a device to read it.

### 1.6 | Which printer suits a busy office printing hundreds of pages, and why?
A **laser** printer — fast, sharp text and low cost per page at high volume.

### 1.6 | Why is a dot matrix printer still used for invoices and payslips?
It strikes the paper, so it prints through multi-part carbon forms in one pass.
No other printer type can do this.

### 1.6 | Which output device suits very large architectural drawings?
A **plotter**, which draws accurately on very large paper.

### 1.6 | Name three devices that are both input and output.
A touchscreen, a multifunction printer, a headset. (A modem also qualifies.)

### 1.6 | Which input device suits a public information kiosk, and why?
A **touchscreen** — it needs no instructions, has no separate parts to be
stolen or damaged, and anyone can use it.

## Questions

### 1.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which input device is most suitable for a supermarket checkout?

- A. Keyboard
- B. Barcode reader
- C. Scanner
- D. Graphics tablet

**Answer:** B

**Working:** A barcode reader is fast and accurate, removing the errors that
typing product codes would cause.

### 1.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
An examination board must mark 40 000 multiple-choice answer sheets. The most
suitable input method is

- A. OCR
- B. OMR
- C. MICR
- D. a keyboard

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Optical mark recognition reads pencil marks in boxes at high speed
— exactly what an answer sheet is. OCR reads characters, and MICR is for
cheques.

### 1.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which input method do banks use to read the numbers along the bottom of a
cheque?

- A. OMR
- B. OCR
- C. MICR
- D. Barcode reading

**Answer:** C

**Working:** Magnetic ink character recognition is fast and highly accurate,
and the characters remain readable even when the cheque is creased or marked.

### 1.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A company prints 200 multi-part invoices a day, each with two carbon copies
beneath it. The most suitable printer is

- A. laser
- B. inkjet
- C. dot matrix
- D. thermal

**Answer:** C

**Working:** A dot matrix printer strikes the paper, so the impact marks all
the copies in one pass. Laser, inkjet and thermal printers never touch the
sheets beneath.

### 1.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
In an automated greenhouse, the computer reads the temperature and opens a vent
when it is too hot. Which pair of devices is being used?

- A. A sensor for input and an actuator for output
- B. A scanner for input and a plotter for output
- C. A keyboard for input and a monitor for output
- D. An actuator for input and a sensor for output

**Answer:** A

**Working:** The sensor measures the temperature and supplies data; the
actuator turns the decision into movement. Option D reverses them.

### 1.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which of the following produces **hard copy**?

- A. A monitor
- B. A printer
- C. Speakers
- D. A projector

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Hard copy is permanent physical output on paper. Monitors and
projectors produce soft copy, and speakers produce sound.

### 1.6 | structured | 10 marks | difficulty 3
A hospital is installing a new computer system. It must record patients'
details at reception, read printed referral letters into the system, restrict
access to the drug store to authorised staff, monitor patients' heart rates
continuously, and print prescriptions.

For each requirement, name a suitable device and give a reason:

(a) recording patients' details at reception **(2 marks)**

(b) reading printed referral letters into the system **(2 marks)**

(c) restricting access to the drug store **(2 marks)**

(d) monitoring heart rates continuously **(2 marks)**

(e) printing prescriptions **(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) 1 mark — a keyboard; 1 mark — the details are text typed in by
the receptionist, and a keyboard enters text accurately.
(b) 1 mark — a scanner (with OCR software); 1 mark — it converts the printed
letter into a file, and OCR turns the printed characters into editable text.
(c) 1 mark — a biometric reader, such as a fingerprint scanner; 1 mark — it
identifies the individual and cannot be lent, forgotten or guessed as a key or
password can.
(d) 1 mark — a sensor (a heart-rate or pulse sensor); 1 mark — it measures
continuously and sends readings automatically, without a nurse present.
(e) 1 mark — a laser printer; 1 mark — it produces sharp, clear text quickly,
which matters because a misread prescription is dangerous. (Accept an inkjet
with a reason about lower volume or cost.)

**Working:** The device alone is worth one mark in each part; the reason is the
other. Tie every reason to the hospital: accuracy in (a) and (e), continuous
unattended measurement in (d), and identity that cannot be transferred in (c).
