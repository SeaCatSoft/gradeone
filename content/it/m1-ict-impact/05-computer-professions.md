## Lesson

Computing is not one job. The roles below differ in what they produce, who they
work with, and what they need to know — and examination questions ask you to
match a role to a described task.

### Building software

**Programmer (developer, coder)** — writes, tests and maintains the code of
programs, working from a design. Needs fluency in one or more programming
languages, and the problem-solving skills of Section 7.

**Systems analyst** — studies an organisation's existing system, identifies its
problems and requirements, and designs a new or improved system for programmers
to build. The bridge between the users and the technical staff.

The distinction is examined: **the analyst decides what the system must do; the
programmer makes it do it.** The analyst spends much of the time talking to
people who are not technical, which is why communication matters as much as
technical knowledge.

**Software engineer** — applies engineering discipline to the whole process of
producing software: design, construction, testing and maintenance, usually of
large systems.

**Web developer** — builds and maintains websites and web applications.
Sometimes split into **front-end** (what the user sees) and **back-end** (the
server and database behind it).

**Software tester / quality assurance** — tests software methodically to find
faults before release, designing the test data and recording results.

**Mobile application developer** — builds applications for phones and tablets.

### Running systems

**Network administrator (network engineer)** — sets up and maintains the
organisation's network: cabling, switches, routers, connections, user accounts
and network security. Keeps the network running.

**Systems administrator** — maintains the servers and operating systems:
installing software, applying updates, managing accounts, taking backups,
monitoring performance.

**Database administrator (DBA)** — designs, maintains and secures the
organisation's databases. Controls who may access what, tunes performance,
ensures backups, and preserves data integrity.

**Computer technician** — installs, services and repairs hardware. Diagnoses
faults, replaces components, and does the troubleshooting of objective 1.10.

**IT support / help desk technician** — the first point of contact for users
with problems. Resolves what they can and passes on what they cannot.
Communication and patience matter at least as much as technical knowledge,
because the user is usually frustrated and often unable to describe the
problem.

**Cybersecurity specialist** — protects systems from attack: monitors for
intrusions, tests defences, responds to incidents, advises on policy.

### Working with data and information

**Data entry clerk** — enters data into the system accurately, working from
source documents. Accuracy matters more than speed, for the GIGO reason.

**Data analyst** — examines data to find patterns and produce information that
supports decisions.

**Database designer** — designs the structure of a database: the tables,
fields, keys and relationships of Section 6.

**Data protection officer** — ensures the organisation handles personal data
lawfully.

### Design and content

**Web designer** — designs the appearance, layout and usability of websites, as
distinct from the developer who builds them.

**Graphic designer** — produces visual material: logos, illustrations, layouts.

**Multimedia developer** — combines text, images, audio, video and animation.

**Technical writer** — produces the documentation of objective 8.6: user
manuals, help pages, technical documentation.

### Management

**IT manager** — manages the IT department: staff, budgets, priorities and
strategy.

**Project manager** — plans and runs a particular project, keeping it to time
and budget and coordinating the people on it.

**Chief information officer (CIO)** — the senior executive responsible for the
organisation's information systems and how technology supports its goals.

### Teaching and research

**IT teacher or trainer**, and **computer scientist / researcher**, developing
new methods and technologies.

### Matching a role to a task

Questions describe a situation and ask who is needed:

| The organisation needs | The role |
|---|---|
| To find out what a new system should do | Systems analyst |
| To write the program | Programmer |
| To design the tables, keys and relationships | Database designer |
| To control who may access which records | Database administrator |
| To connect the offices and keep the network running | Network administrator |
| To repair a computer that will not start | Computer technician |
| To help a user who cannot print | IT support technician |
| To find faults before the software is released | Software tester |
| To write the user manual | Technical writer |
| To investigate a suspected intrusion | Cybersecurity specialist |
| To keep the project to time and budget | Project manager |
| To make the website easy to use and attractive | Web designer |

### Skills these roles share

Technical knowledge is assumed. What separates people within a role:

- **Communication**, especially with non-technical users. The analyst and the
  support technician spend most of their time on it.
- **Problem-solving** — the method of Section 7 applied to real situations.
- **Attention to detail**, since a single character can break a program.
- **Continuous learning.** Technology changes constantly, and a qualification
  earned once does not last a career.
- **Teamwork**, because almost nothing is built alone.
- **Ethics and confidentiality.** These roles come with access to other
  people's data, and the trust that goes with it.

## Flashcards

### 3.5 | What does a systems analyst do?
Studies an organisation's existing system, identifies its problems and
requirements, and designs a new or improved system.

### 3.5 | Distinguish between a systems analyst and a programmer.
The **analyst** decides what the system must do; the **programmer** writes the
code that makes it do it.

> Hint: which one spends the day talking to non-technical users?

### 3.5 | What does a programmer do?
Writes, tests and maintains the code of programs, working from a design.

### 3.5 | What does a network administrator do?
Sets up and maintains the organisation's network — cabling, switches, routers,
connections, user accounts and network security.

### 3.5 | What does a database administrator do?
Designs, maintains and secures the organisation's databases: controls access,
tunes performance, ensures backups and preserves data integrity.

### 3.5 | What does a computer technician do?
Installs, services and repairs hardware — diagnosing faults and replacing
components.

### 3.5 | What does an IT support technician do, and which non-technical skill matters most?
Acts as the first point of contact for users with problems. **Communication and
patience**, since the user is usually frustrated and often cannot describe the
fault.

### 3.5 | What does a software tester do?
Tests software methodically to find faults before it is released, designing
test data and recording the results.

### 3.5 | What does a cybersecurity specialist do?
Protects systems from attack — monitoring for intrusions, testing defences,
responding to incidents and advising on policy.

### 3.5 | What is the difference between a web designer and a web developer?
The **designer** decides the appearance, layout and usability; the
**developer** builds the site and the systems behind it.

### 3.5 | What does a technical writer produce?
The documentation of a system — user manuals, help pages and technical
documentation.

### 3.5 | What does a project manager do?
Plans and runs a particular project, keeping it to time and budget and
coordinating the people working on it.

### 3.5 | Which role would an organisation need to decide what a new system should do?
A **systems analyst**.

### 3.5 | Name four skills shared across computer-related professions.
Any four: communication with non-technical users; problem-solving; attention to
detail; continuous learning; teamwork; ethics and confidentiality.

### 3.5 | Why is continuous learning essential in computing careers?
Technology changes constantly, so a qualification earned once does not last a
whole career.

## Questions

### 3.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Who studies an organisation's existing system and determines the requirements
for a new one?

- A. A programmer
- B. A systems analyst
- C. A computer technician
- D. A data entry clerk

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The analyst investigates and designs; the programmer then builds
what the analyst specified.

### 3.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A company's printer will not work and a user telephones for help. Who deals
with this first?

- A. The database administrator
- B. The IT support technician
- C. The systems analyst
- D. The project manager

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Support is the first point of contact for users with problems,
resolving what they can and passing on what they cannot.

### 3.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Who is responsible for controlling which staff may access which records in an
organisation's database?

- A. The network administrator
- B. The database administrator
- C. The web developer
- D. The technical writer

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Access control, performance, backups and data integrity for the
database are the DBA's responsibility.

### 3.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Which role is chiefly responsible for finding faults in software **before** it
is released?

- A. The programmer
- B. The software tester
- C. The systems analyst
- D. The IT manager

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Testers design test data and check the software methodically.
Programmers test their own work too, but an independent tester is more likely
to find what the author overlooked.

### 3.5 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Which pair of roles is most likely to spend the largest share of their time
communicating with people who are **not** technical?

- A. Programmer and database administrator
- B. Systems analyst and IT support technician
- C. Network administrator and software tester
- D. Web developer and technician

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The analyst gathers requirements from users, and support deals
with users in difficulty. Both need communication as much as technical
knowledge.

### 3.5 | structured | 9 marks | difficulty 3
A company is replacing its manual stock system with a computerised one.

(a) Name the person who would investigate the current system and decide what
the new one must do, and state **two** of their duties. **(3 marks)**

(b) Name the person who would write the software, and state **one** skill they
need. **(2 marks)**

(c) Name the person who would set up and maintain the connections between the
company's offices. **(1 mark)**

(d) Name the person who would control access to the new stock database.
**(1 mark)**

(e) Once the system is running, staff will need help using it. Name the role
responsible and state **one** non-technical skill it requires. **(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) 1 mark — a systems analyst; 2 marks for any two duties: study
the existing system; identify its problems; gather requirements from users;
design the new system; specify what the programmers must build; evaluate the
completed system.
(b) 1 mark — a programmer (or software developer); 1 mark — a skill such as
fluency in a programming language, problem-solving, or attention to detail.
(c) A network administrator — 1 mark.
(d) A database administrator — 1 mark.
(e) 1 mark — an IT support or help desk technician; 1 mark — communication or
patience, since users are often frustrated and cannot describe the problem
precisely.

**Working:** (a) and (b) test the same distinction from two sides: the analyst
determines **what**, the programmer implements **how**. Confusing them is the
commonest error on this objective.
