## Lesson

The syllabus names **five** fields for this objective, and gives the aspects it
expects for each. They are worth learning as a set, because a question naming
one of them expects the terms listed under it.

| Field | Aspects named |
|---|---|
| **Education** | Access to information, reach (distance teaching), collaborative teaching and learning, plagiarism, online tutoring |
| **Medicine** | Access to information, telemedicine, eHealth, quality of healthcare, self-diagnosis, expertise at a distance (teleradiology) |
| **Business** | E-commerce, electronic point of sale (EPOS), telecommuting, email |
| **Law enforcement** | E-surveillance, fingerprinting, biometrics |
| **Recreation** | Music and gaming |

Throughout, the objective says *assess* — so each field has costs as well as
benefits.

### Education

**Access to information.** A student with a connection can reach more material
than any school library holds. The gain is real; so is the cost, since the
material is unfiltered, which is why judging online sources (objective 1.12)
matters.

**Reach — distance teaching.** Lessons reach students who cannot attend in
person: those on other islands, in remote districts, in work, or unable to
travel. A school can offer a subject it has no teacher for.

**Collaborative teaching and learning.** Students work together on shared
documents; teachers share resources; classes in different countries work on the
same project.

**Online tutoring.** One-to-one teaching without either person travelling,
which widens the choice of tutor beyond whoever lives nearby.

**Plagiarism.** The syllabus names this explicitly as an impact. Copying has
become trivially easy, so schools now use detection software and teach
citation. The problem is not only dishonesty: a student who copies has not
learnt the material, and finds out in the examination.

Other effects: administration, records and reporting are computerised; teachers
need continual retraining; and the **digital divide** decides who benefits,
since a student without a device or a connection is now excluded from work that
was previously available to everyone.

### Medicine

**Access to information**, for both staff and patients. A doctor can consult
current research and drug interactions in moments. Patients arrive informed —
which can help, and can mislead.

**Telemedicine.** Consulting a doctor remotely by video. It reaches rural
communities and other islands, saves travel, and lets a local doctor consult a
specialist during the appointment rather than referring the patient onward.

**eHealth** — online access to health services: booking appointments, viewing
records, repeat prescriptions, test results.

**Teleradiology** — a scan taken in one place is read by a specialist in
another, possibly in another country. This is the clearest case of **expertise
at a distance**: a hospital with no resident radiologist can still have images
read by one within the hour.

**Quality of healthcare.** Computerised records are legible, searchable,
available instantly and in several places at once; they reduce prescribing
errors and drug interactions. Monitoring equipment watches patients
continuously, and imaging and robot-assisted surgery are computer-controlled.

**Self-diagnosis.** Named explicitly, and the assessment is genuinely mixed.
People who understand their symptoms sooner may seek help earlier. But people
also misdiagnose themselves, take unnecessary alarm, delay treatment, or reject
a doctor's advice in favour of what they read. Search results are not ordered
by medical authority.

Costs elsewhere: a breach of medical records is among the most damaging
possible; systems fail, and ransomware in a hospital is a threat to life; and
equipment is expensive to buy and maintain.

### Business

**E-commerce** — buying and selling online. A small business can reach
customers worldwide without premises; customers shop at any hour and compare
prices. Against that: physical shops lose trade, fraud is easier, and goods
cannot be examined before purchase.

**EPOS — electronic point of sale.** The barcode-reading till that is far more
than a till: it prices the item, prints the receipt, takes payment, updates
stock immediately, and records what sold when. The manager gets sales analysis
without counting anything, and reordering can be automatic. Fewer pricing
errors, faster queues, fewer staff.

**Telecommuting** — working from home or elsewhere, connected to the office.
The employee saves travel time and cost and gains flexibility; the employer
saves office space and can recruit anywhere. Against: isolation, blurred
boundaries between work and home, supervision at a distance, and a dependence
on the employee's own connection.

**Email** — near-instant written communication, cheap, keeping a record, and
reaching anywhere. It also brings overload, the expectation of immediate
replies, spam and phishing.

Other effects: stock control, payroll and accounting are computerised; online
banking and payment; advertising has moved to search and social media; and
automation has changed which jobs exist (objective 3.4).

### Law enforcement

**E-surveillance.** CCTV, automatic number-plate recognition, and monitoring of
communications. It deters offences, provides evidence and helps locate
suspects. It also watches everyone, most of whom have done nothing, which is
the privacy objection — and the assessment expected here is the balance between
public safety and individual privacy.

**Fingerprinting.** Fingerprints are digitised and searched automatically
against a national database in moments, where a manual search once took weeks.
Prints from a scene can be matched to records held anywhere in the system.

**Biometrics** more broadly — face recognition, iris and voice — used to
identify suspects, control access, and verify identity at borders. Face
recognition raises the sharpest questions, because it can identify people at a
distance without their knowledge or consent, and because it has been shown to
be less accurate for some groups than others, which makes a wrong match more
likely for them.

Other effects: national criminal records, crime statistics collected and
analysed to direct patrols, digital forensics recovering evidence from devices,
and computerised dispatch. Against them: the cost, the training required, the
risk of a breach of police data, and the fact that computerised records make
an error — a wrong entry — follow a person indefinitely.

### Recreation

**Music.** Recording, production and distribution are all digital. Streaming
put a vast catalogue within reach of anyone with a connection, and let artists
publish without a record company. Against that, revenue per play is small, so
the economics changed for musicians, and piracy is trivially easy.

**Gaming.** Games are a major industry and, increasingly, a social activity —
played with people in other countries, watched by audiences, and played
competitively for money. Benefits: entertainment, coordination and
problem-solving skills, social contact for people who are isolated. Costs:
excessive play displacing sleep, study and exercise; spending built into games;
contact with strangers; and the health effects of long sedentary sessions.

Other recreation effects: streamed film and television; digital photography;
social media; and online booking for travel and events.

### Answering this objective

Questions name a field and ask for the impact. The structure that scores:

1. **Use the syllabus's own terms** for that field — `telemedicine`, `EPOS`,
   `e-surveillance`. They are what the mark scheme is looking for.
2. Give **benefits with a mechanism**: not "email is faster" but "email
   delivers in seconds to anywhere in the world, so a supplier abroad can be
   answered the same day".
3. Give **drawbacks**, because the verb is *assess*.
4. **Conclude** where the question asks you to weigh them.

A common theme is worth carrying across all five fields: ICT removes the
importance of **distance**. The specialist reading a scan, the tutor, the
customer, the employee working from home and the player in another country are
all people whose location stopped mattering. Most of the benefits in this
lesson follow from that, and so do several of the costs.

## Questions

### 3.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A doctor in a rural clinic consults a specialist in the capital by video while
the patient is present. This is an example of

- A. eHealth
- B. telemedicine
- C. e-surveillance
- D. EPOS

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Telemedicine is medical consultation carried out remotely. eHealth
refers to online access to health services such as booking and records.

### 3.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
An EPOS system at a supermarket checkout does all of the following **except**

- A. update stock levels as items are sold
- B. record which products sold and when
- C. diagnose faults in the store's network
- D. print an itemised receipt

**Answer:** C

**Working:** EPOS handles pricing, payment, receipts, stock and sales data.
Network diagnosis is not part of it.

### 3.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which is listed in the syllabus as an impact of ICT on **education**?

- A. EPOS
- B. Plagiarism
- C. Teleradiology
- D. Biometrics

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The syllabus names access to information, reach, collaborative
learning, plagiarism and online tutoring under education. The others belong to
business, medicine and law enforcement.

### 3.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A scan taken at a small hospital is read by a specialist in another country
within the hour. This is

- A. eHealth
- B. teleradiology
- C. self-diagnosis
- D. e-surveillance

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Teleradiology is the reading of medical images at a distance, and
it lets a hospital with no resident radiologist still obtain a specialist
opinion quickly.

### 3.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Which is the strongest argument **against** widespread e-surveillance?

- A. It deters crime
- B. It monitors everyone, including the great majority who have done nothing
- C. It provides evidence for prosecutions
- D. It helps locate suspects

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The other three are arguments in its favour. The privacy objection
is that surveillance is applied to the whole population rather than to
suspects.

### 3.6 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
An employee works from home three days a week, connected to the company's
systems. This is

- A. e-commerce
- B. telecommuting
- C. telemedicine
- D. eHealth

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Telecommuting is working away from the office while connected to
it. E-commerce is buying and selling online.

### 3.6 | structured | 10 marks | difficulty 3
Assess the impact of information and communications technology on **medicine**
and on **business**.

(a) Describe **two** ways ICT has improved the delivery of healthcare, using
the correct terms. **(4 marks)**

(b) State **one** drawback of patients having easy access to medical
information online. **(2 marks)**

(c) Explain how an **EPOS** system benefits a supermarket. **(2 marks)**

(d) State **one** benefit and **one** drawback of **telecommuting**.
**(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) 2 marks each for two developed points: **telemedicine** —
patients consult a doctor remotely by video, reaching rural communities and
saving travel, and a local doctor can consult a specialist during the
appointment; **teleradiology** — a scan is read by a specialist elsewhere, so a
hospital with no resident radiologist still obtains an expert opinion quickly;
**computerised records** — legible, searchable and instantly available in
several places, reducing prescribing errors; **eHealth** — patients book
appointments, request prescriptions and view results online.
(b) 2 marks: patients may **self-diagnose** incorrectly — taking unnecessary
alarm, delaying proper treatment, or rejecting a doctor's advice in favour of
what they have read, since search results are not ordered by medical authority.
(c) 2 marks: the system prices items and takes payment quickly, and updates
stock levels as each sale is recorded, so reordering can be automatic and the
manager obtains sales analysis without counting stock.
(d) 1 mark — a benefit, such as the employee saving travel time and cost, or
the employer saving office space and recruiting from anywhere; 1 mark — a
drawback, such as isolation, blurred boundaries between work and home, harder
supervision, or dependence on the employee's own connection.

**Working:** (a) The named terms carry the marks. Describing remote
consultation without using the word *telemedicine* scores less than an answer
that names it and then explains it.

(b) Self-diagnosis is the answer the syllabus expects here — it lists it
explicitly among the impacts on medicine.
