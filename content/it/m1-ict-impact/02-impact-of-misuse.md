## Lesson

The previous lesson described what misuse *is*. This one is about who it harms
and how badly — and the syllabus organises it by the **entities impacted**.

Three entities: the **individual**, the **organisation**, and **society** (or
the nation). An answer that works through all three is structured; one that
lists harms at random is not.

### Impact on the individual

**Financial loss.** Money taken from accounts, fraudulent purchases on a stolen
card, payment to a scam. Some is recoverable and some is not.

**Identity theft.** Somebody impersonates you: opens accounts, takes loans,
commits offences in your name. The financial loss may be the smallest part of
it. Clearing a damaged credit record takes months or years, and the burden of
proving you are the victim falls on you.

**Loss of privacy.** Personal messages, photographs, medical or financial
details exposed. Once published, information cannot be recalled.

**Loss of data.** Irreplaceable photographs and documents destroyed by
ransomware or a wiped device.

**Emotional harm.** Cyberbullying and cyberstalking cause anxiety, isolation
and depression, and at their worst have contributed to suicides. It is
relentless in a way that earlier bullying was not: it follows the victim home,
it can be anonymous, and it can be seen by an unlimited audience.

**Damage to reputation.** A false accusation or a leaked private image spreads
faster than any correction.

**Loss of time**, dealing with the consequences.

**Physical danger**, where a stalker uses location data, or where medical
devices and records are interfered with.

### Impact on the organisation

**Direct financial loss** — money stolen, ransoms paid, fraud.

**Cost of recovery** — investigating the breach, restoring systems, replacing
hardware, paying specialists. Frequently larger than the theft itself.

**Downtime.** A system unavailable for a day costs a day's trading. For a bank
or an airline the figure is enormous, and it accumulates whether or not
anything was stolen.

**Loss of data**, including customer records and years of work.

**Loss of customer confidence.** A company that loses its customers' data loses
its customers. This is often the most serious consequence, and the hardest to
undo, because trust is slow to rebuild.

**Damage to reputation**, which affects sales, partnerships and recruitment.

**Legal liability.** Data protection legislation obliges organisations to
protect personal data. A breach can bring fines, lawsuits and regulatory
action.

**Loss of competitive advantage**, where designs, client lists or research are
stolen.

**Higher insurance and security costs** afterwards.

**Staff effects** — stress, blame, sometimes dismissal.

### Impact on society and the nation

**Attacks on critical infrastructure.** Power, water, hospitals, transport and
telecommunications are all computer-controlled. An attack on them threatens
lives, not just money — a hospital unable to reach patient records during an
emergency is the clearest case.

**Economic damage.** Cybercrime removes money from an economy, raises the cost
of doing business, and deters investment.

**Loss of trust in digital services.** If people do not trust online banking,
government services or commerce, the benefits of those systems are lost to
everyone — including those who were never attacked.

**Threats to national security**, where state secrets or defence systems are
targeted.

**The spread of false information**, which can affect public health decisions
and elections.

**Cost to the public purse** — policing, investigation, and defending
government systems.

**The digital divide widens** when people withdraw from technology out of fear.

### Assessing severity

The objective says *assess*, so questions expect judgement, not a list. Four
things determine how bad an incident is:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| **What was affected** | Money, personal data, or safety? |
| **How many** | One person, ten thousand customers, a country? |
| **Reversibility** | Can it be undone? |
| **Duration** | Minutes, or years? |

**Reversibility** is the one students underrate. Stolen money can often be
refunded. Leaked personal data cannot be un-leaked — it exists somewhere for
ever, and can be used years later. That is why a data breach is frequently
graver than a theft of the same monetary value, and saying so in an answer
shows the assessment the objective is asking for.

### Worked example

*A hospital is hit by ransomware. Its patient records are encrypted and the
appointment system is unavailable for four days.*

**Individuals** — patients: appointments and operations cancelled; treatment
delayed; staff unable to see allergies or medication history, so care becomes
unsafe; anxiety and, at worst, harm to health.

**Organisation** — the hospital: cost of recovery; a ransom decision; four days
of lost operations; possible legal liability if records were also stolen; loss
of public confidence; staff under extreme pressure.

**Society** — the health system: other hospitals absorb the load; public trust
in digital health records falls; the cost falls on the public purse; the
attack demonstrates a vulnerability others may copy.

**Severity** — very high: it affects safety rather than money, touches
thousands, and delayed treatment cannot be undone.

That structure — the three entities, then a judgement of severity — answers
almost any question on this objective.

## Flashcards

### 3.2 | Name the three entities to consider when assessing the impact of computer misuse.
The **individual**, the **organisation**, and **society** or the nation.

### 3.2 | Give four impacts of computer misuse on an individual.
Any four: financial loss; identity theft; loss of privacy; loss of
irreplaceable data; emotional harm from cyberbullying; damage to reputation;
time spent on the consequences; physical danger.

### 3.2 | Why is identity theft often worse than the money lost?
Clearing a damaged credit record takes months or years, the burden of proving
you are the victim falls on you, and offences may be committed in your name.

### 3.2 | Why is cyberbullying more damaging than earlier forms of bullying?
It follows the victim home rather than ending at the school gate, it can be
anonymous, and it can be seen by an unlimited audience.

### 3.2 | Give five impacts of computer misuse on an organisation.
Any five: direct financial loss; the cost of recovery; downtime; loss of data;
loss of customer confidence; reputational damage; legal liability under data
protection law; loss of competitive advantage.

### 3.2 | Which consequence of a data breach is often the most serious for a company, and why?
**Loss of customer confidence** — a company that loses its customers' data
loses its customers, and trust is slow to rebuild.

### 3.2 | Why does downtime cost an organisation even if nothing is stolen?
A system unavailable for a day costs a day's trading, and for a bank or airline
that figure is enormous.

### 3.2 | Give four impacts of computer misuse on society.
Any four: attacks on critical infrastructure threatening lives; economic
damage; loss of trust in digital services; threats to national security; the
spread of false information; cost to the public purse.

### 3.2 | Why is an attack on critical infrastructure especially serious?
Power, water, hospitals and transport are computer-controlled, so an attack
threatens lives rather than only money.

### 3.2 | Name four factors determining how severe an incident is.
What was affected; how many people; whether it can be reversed; and how long it
lasts.

### 3.2 | Why is a data breach often graver than a theft of the same monetary value?
Stolen money can often be refunded, but leaked personal data cannot be
un-leaked — it exists somewhere for ever and may be used years later.

## Questions

### 3.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which is the most serious long-term consequence for a company whose customer
database is stolen?

- A. The cost of replacing the hard discs
- B. Loss of customer confidence and reputation
- C. The time spent restarting the servers
- D. The cost of the investigation

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The direct costs are bounded and one-off. Lost trust affects every
future sale, and rebuilding it takes years.

### 3.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Why is leaked personal data often considered worse than stolen money?

- A. Personal data is worth more
- B. Stolen money can often be refunded, but leaked data cannot be recalled
- C. Money is not protected by law
- D. Data breaches are always larger

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Reversibility is the deciding factor. Leaked data exists somewhere
permanently and may be used years afterwards.

### 3.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A ransomware attack on a hospital delays operations for four days. The entity
most seriously affected is

- A. the software supplier
- B. the patients, whose treatment is delayed and whose safety is at risk
- C. the hospital's internet provider
- D. the attacker

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The question asks who is most seriously affected. Financial harm
to the hospital is real, but the patients' safety outweighs it.

### 3.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Which impact of computer misuse falls on **society** rather than on an
individual or a single organisation?

- A. A stolen credit card
- B. A company paying a ransom
- C. Loss of public trust in online banking
- D. An employee losing their job after a breach

**Answer:** C

**Working:** When people stop trusting digital services, the benefits are lost
to everyone — including those never attacked. The others affect one person or
one organisation.

### 3.2 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
An airline's booking system is unavailable for six hours although no data is
stolen. The main impact is

- A. nothing, since no data was taken
- B. downtime — six hours of lost bookings and disrupted passengers
- C. identity theft
- D. loss of competitive advantage only

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Availability is one of the three security goals in its own right.
A system that cannot be used causes loss whether or not anything is taken.

### 3.2 | structured | 10 marks | difficulty 4
A secondary school's computer system is attacked. Student records, including
addresses and medical information, are copied and published online. The school
network is unusable for a week.

(a) Describe **two** impacts on the students and their families. **(4 marks)**

(b) Describe **two** impacts on the school as an organisation. **(4 marks)**

(c) Assess which of these impacts is the most serious, giving a reason.
**(2 marks)**

**Answer:** (a) 2 marks each for two developed impacts: loss of privacy, since
addresses and medical details are now public and cannot be recalled; risk of
identity theft or fraud using the personal details; distress and anxiety;
physical risk where an address exposes a family that needed to remain private.
(b) 2 marks each for two developed impacts: a week of downtime, with teaching,
registers and reporting disrupted; the cost of investigating and restoring the
system; legal liability for failing to protect personal data; loss of
confidence among parents; reputational damage affecting future enrolment.
(c) 1 mark for a clear judgement; 1 mark for a reason. A strong answer chooses
the publication of the personal data, because the week of downtime is temporary
and recoverable while published information cannot be withdrawn and may be
misused for years.

**Working:** (c) The mark is for the reasoning, not the choice. The test that
earns it is **reversibility**: the network comes back, and the data never
becomes private again.
