## Lesson

Anyone can publish a website, and nobody has to check it first. Judging a site
is therefore a skill you need both as a **visitor**, deciding whether to
believe what you read, and as a **builder**, deciding whether what you made is
good enough.

The syllabus names three criteria: **accuracy**, **user friendliness** and
**effective display**.

### Accuracy

Is the information correct, current and trustworthy?

**Authority.** Who wrote it? A named author or a recognised organisation can be
held responsible; an anonymous page cannot. Look at the domain: an official
body, a university or an established news organisation carries more weight than
a personal blog.

**Currency.** When was it published or last updated? A page with no date is a
warning in itself. On a school site, a "latest news" item from three years ago
tells visitors the site is abandoned, whatever else it says.

**Evidence.** Are claims supported? Are sources cited, and do the links to them
work?

**Objectivity.** What is the site *for*? A page arguing that a product is the
best available, published by the company selling it, is advertising. That does
not make it false, but it does mean checking elsewhere.

**Verifiability.** Can the facts be confirmed from an independent source? For
anything surprising or important, check a second place before repeating it.

**Correctness of detail.** Spelling and grammar. Errors do not make the
information wrong, but they say something about the care taken — and visitors
judge a site by them.

### User friendliness

Can a visitor find what they came for, easily, on whatever device they are
using?

**Navigation.** Is there a clear menu on every page, in the same position? Can
you get back to the home page from anywhere? Is it obvious where you are?

**Findability.** Is the content organised as a visitor would expect? Can
anything important be reached in about three clicks? Is there a search box on a
large site?

**Working links.** Do all of them work, or do some give `404 Not Found`?

**Speed.** Does the page load quickly, including on mobile data? Oversized
images are the usual culprit.

**Mobile.** Does it work at phone width — readable text, tappable links, no
sideways scrolling? Most visitors arrive this way.

**Clear language**, pitched at the intended audience, with headings and short
paragraphs rather than unbroken text.

**Helpful feedback.** Do forms say what went wrong? Does a missing page offer a
way onward, or just an error?

**Accessibility.** Can it be used by someone who cannot see the screen or use a
mouse? Alternative text on images, sufficient colour contrast, text that can be
enlarged, headings marking the structure, everything reachable by keyboard.
Accessibility is part of user friendliness, not a separate favour.

### Effective display

Does the site look right, and does its appearance help rather than hinder?

**Consistency.** The same layout, colours, fonts and menu throughout.

**Readability.** Strong contrast between text and background, a large enough
font, sensible line length. Pale grey text on white, or text over a patterned
background, fails here.

**Restraint in fonts and colours.** Two or three fonts; a small palette used
consistently. A page in five typefaces looks like an accident.

**Purposeful images.** Images that add something, sized properly and not
stretched out of proportion.

**White space.** Room around text and between sections. A crowded page is
tiring before it is read.

**Balance and alignment.** Elements lined up, with weight distributed across
the page.

**Nothing that gets in the way.** No automatically playing audio or video, no
text that flashes or scrolls, no pop-up before the visitor has seen anything.
Each of these was once fashionable and each drives people away.

### Evaluating with a checklist

An evaluation is worth more when it is written down and scored, so that two
people assessing the same site can compare, and so that improvement can be
measured.

| Criterion | Question | Score /5 |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Is the information correct, current and sourced? | |
| Authority | Is the author or organisation identified? | |
| Navigation | Is the menu clear and on every page? | |
| Links | Do they all work? | |
| Speed | Does it load quickly on mobile data? | |
| Mobile | Does it work at phone width? | |
| Accessibility | Alt text, contrast, keyboard use? | |
| Consistency | Same layout and colours throughout? | |
| Readability | Good contrast and font size? | |
| Purpose | Does it do what it set out to do? | |

The last row matters most and is the one most often left out. A site can score
well on every technical point and still fail, because nobody can do the thing
it was built for.

### Testing your own site

Before publishing:

- Check **every link**, including external ones.
- View it in **more than one browser**, and at **phone width**.
- Run a **spelling check** on the content.
- Ask **someone else** to find something specific on it, and watch without
  helping. Where they hesitate is a design fault, not their mistake.
- Check the pages load quickly.
- Confirm images have **alt text**.
- Check it again **after publishing**, since links that worked locally can
  break on the server.

The most useful of these is watching someone else use it. You cannot judge your
own site's navigation, because you already know where everything is.

## Flashcards

### 4.12 | Name the three criteria for evaluating a website given in the syllabus.
**Accuracy**, **user friendliness** and **effective display**.

### 4.12 | Name four things to check when judging a website's accuracy.
Any four: who the author or organisation is; when it was last updated; whether
claims are supported and sources cited; whether it is objective or promotional;
whether the facts can be confirmed elsewhere.

### 4.12 | Why does the absence of a date on a web page matter?
There is no way to tell whether the information is current. Out-of-date
material can be as misleading as wrong material.

### 4.12 | Why treat a company's page about its own product with caution?
Its purpose is to sell. It is not necessarily false, but it is not objective,
so claims should be checked against an independent source.

### 4.12 | Name four things to check when judging user friendliness.
Any four: a clear menu on every page; content organised as visitors expect;
all links working; quick loading; usable at phone width; clear language;
helpful error messages; accessibility.

### 4.12 | Why is accessibility part of user friendliness?
A site that cannot be used by someone who cannot see the screen or use a mouse
is not usable by everyone who needs it.

### 4.12 | Name four features of effective display.
Any four: consistency of layout and colour; strong contrast and readable font
size; restraint in fonts and colours; purposeful, properly sized images;
adequate white space; good alignment and balance.

### 4.12 | Name three things that harm a site even though they are technically features.
Automatically playing audio or video; flashing or scrolling text; pop-ups
appearing before the visitor has seen anything.

### 4.12 | Why is a checklist with scores better than a general impression?
Two evaluators can compare their results, and improvement can be measured
rather than argued about.

### 4.12 | Which evaluation question is most often left out, and why does it matter?
Whether the site achieves its **purpose**. A site can score well on every
technical point and still fail if nobody can do what it was built for.

### 4.12 | Why ask someone else to find something on your site?
You already know where everything is, so you cannot judge your own navigation.
Where they hesitate is a design fault.

### 4.12 | Name two checks to make after publishing that were already made before.
Check every link, and view the site in more than one browser and at phone
width. Links that worked locally can break on the server.

## Questions

### 4.12 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 1
Which three criteria does the syllabus give for evaluating a website?

- A. Speed, cost and colour
- B. Accuracy, user friendliness and effective display
- C. Length, images and links
- D. HTML, CSS and JavaScript

**Answer:** B

**Working:** These are the three named in objective 4.12, and an evaluation
question expects them.

### 4.12 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
A student finds a web page with useful statistics but no author's name and no
date. The main concern is

- A. the page may load slowly
- B. there is no way to judge the authority or currency of the information
- C. the page cannot be printed
- D. the page will not work on a phone

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Without an author, nobody is responsible for the claims; without a
date, there is no way to know whether they are still true.

### 4.12 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A company's website states that its product is the best on the market. Which
evaluation criterion does this most directly call into question?

- A. Effective display
- B. Objectivity
- C. Loading speed
- D. Accessibility

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The site exists to sell the product, so it is not a neutral
source. The claim may still be true, but it needs confirming independently.

### 4.12 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 2
Which of the following most affects **user friendliness** rather than
accuracy?

- A. The date the page was updated
- B. Whether sources are cited
- C. Whether the menu appears in the same place on every page
- D. Whether the author is named

**Answer:** C

**Working:** A consistent menu is about finding your way around, which is user
friendliness. The other three are all tests of accuracy and authority.

### 4.12 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
A site uses pale grey text on a white background with a patterned image behind
it. This is a failure of

- A. accuracy
- B. readability, part of effective display
- C. navigation
- D. currency

**Answer:** B

**Working:** The information may be perfectly accurate and the menu perfectly
clear; the problem is that the text cannot comfortably be read. Contrast is
also an accessibility issue.

### 4.12 | mcq | 1 mark | difficulty 3
Why is watching someone else try to find information on your site more useful
than checking it yourself?

- A. They will notice spelling errors you missed
- B. You already know where everything is, so you cannot judge the navigation
- C. It is required before a site can be published
- D. It tests the site's loading speed

**Answer:** B

**Working:** Familiarity hides navigation problems from the person who built
the site. Where a stranger hesitates is where the design has failed.

### 4.12 | structured | 10 marks | difficulty 3
A student has built a website for a local sports club and must evaluate it
before it is handed over.

(a) State the **three** criteria by which a website should be evaluated.
**(3 marks)**

(b) State **two** checks the student should make under **accuracy**, and
explain why each matters. **(4 marks)**

(c) State **two** checks the student should make under **user friendliness**.
**(2 marks)**

(d) The student tests the site only on their own laptop. State **one** further
test that should be carried out, and give a reason. **(1 mark)**

**Answer:** (a) 1 mark each: accuracy; user friendliness; effective display.
(b) 2 marks each for any two, one for the check and one for the reason:
the information is up to date — out-of-date fixtures or contact details
mislead visitors; sources or the author are identified — visitors can judge
whether to trust the claims; spelling and grammar are correct — errors reduce
confidence in the site; all facts are correct, such as dates and times of
matches.
(c) 1 mark each, any two: every page carries the same menu; every link works;
the site loads quickly; it works at phone width; the language suits the
audience; images have alternative text.
(d) Any one, 1 mark: test it at phone width or on a phone, because most
visitors will use one; test it in another browser, because pages can display
differently; ask someone else to find something, because the builder already
knows where everything is; test it again after publishing, because links that
work locally can break on a server.

**Working:** (b) Each check needs a reason for the second mark, and the reason
should be tied to this site. "The information must be accurate" simply restates
the criterion; "out-of-date fixture times would send members to the wrong
place" earns the mark.

(d) Testing only on the machine it was built on misses the two commonest
failures: a layout that breaks on a phone, and links whose capitalisation only
matters once the site is on a server.
